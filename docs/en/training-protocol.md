## 1. Purpose and scope

This is a separate operational companion to the Core hypotheses, with the same importance for experimental execution. The hypotheses define the research question; this protocol defines how to preserve effective methods, develop a continuous subject, calibrate its peripheral and measure actual performance. Implementation convenience must not silently replace teaching, reading or control paths and then assign poor scores to the Core.

The methods already existed across reports, the E64 preflight audit and the retrospective, but lacked a unified execution guide. This document consolidates them at the user's request. Its operational requirements apply to subsequent work; the demonstrated effects of its reference procedures remain bounded by their conditions. E64's old-subject calibration is still unexecuted. Creating this document does not establish that both blank and old subjects work, or that every task will achieve perfect performance.

The protocol covers teaching, recognition, expression, movement and correction involving a Core and learned peripherals as one system. The shape/color parameters below are reusable reference configurations, not universal biological constants. Exact reproduction preserves the original configuration; task adaptation declares its differences; new training methods remain separate from the successful reference. Existing ocean, language and other studies retain their own versions and registrations.

## 3. Subject identity and control path

1. **Continue the same complete system through ordinary stages.** Preserve Core, input queues and clocks, personal peripheral weights and optimizer/cache/random state, body and world, sensory/action mappings, current output, recent readout and curriculum position. Record the parent checkpoint. A Core-only checkpoint is not a whole-system checkpoint.
2. **Continuity applies per individual.** Control groups may differ in architecture or training conditions, while each continues its own state. Another individual's reader is not interchangeable by default. Authorized transplants, cold starts, frozen readers and ablations need separate identities and an intact reference.
3. **Explain consequential changes before execution.** Replacing/reinitializing a peripheral, changing the control path, resetting development or splitting joint learning into independent fitting requires explaining its effect on the question and obtaining user agreement. Existing explicit authorization does not require repeated approval; routine implementation stays within its authorized scope.
4. **Identify what controls each action.** Separate teacher assistance, peripheral selection, servo execution, fixed wiring, wind and inertia. Assisted motion, an already-reached initial position or passive drift does not count as newly learned action; fixed eyes/hands do not establish learned fixation.
5. **Preserve Core and input contracts.** Keep the applicable dynamics, anonymous mapping, ordinary intensity, forgetting, inhibition and physical background. Do not silently alter parameters, disable mechanisms, inject semantic labels or remove the environment for a better score. Approved mechanism interventions use separate copies.

Peripheral fitting on fixed recordings is instrument calibration and does not advance Core. Teaching is external experience and advances Core and its environment. They may alternate under a declared design, but optimization steps are not lessons experienced by Core. Continuous joint development must not be replaced by an undisclosed segmented pipeline. ## 5. Teaching and practice

**Provide the intended experience before testing the registered question.** In the reference shape/color procedure, each concept first receives consecutive demonstrations, then interleaved reviews. Body and environment continue supplying input during intervals. Save the original sentences, objects/pointing cues, start/end times and actual per-concept revisit intervals, not just a curriculum name.

With four simultaneous objects, teaching needs a perceptible pointing cue to disambiguate the referent; remove it during tests. A single centered object does not require copying a teacher light that would obscure it. That is a declared adaptation to the task.

Practice may select later teaching/correction items. Practice records subsequently used for calibration no longer count as final held-out evidence. Formal questions must not secretly supply correct answers or select/update the reader. Once scores inform a method change, those questions can remain development diagnostics but cannot again be described as unseen confirmation.

For correction, record the system's own answer/action, provide normal teacher demonstrations, review earlier correct examples and compare actual performance. E62/E63 support examining retention of old translations and show limited benefits, not a guaranteed recipe for reliable action. Keep answer-bearing correction separate from answer-free matched exposure: E56's final successful source used the latter. Teaching continuity, calibration and checkpoint selection must not all be called correction.

## 7. Reading and peripheral calibration

1. **Sample actual answering conditions.** Do not present the correct answer before querying. Demonstration-aftereffect activity is not interchangeable with answer-free question activity. E56 requests may name the target through ordinary sensory input but supply neither its correct position nor a teacher marker; E64 questions do not supply the specific shape/color to be answered. Teacher labels supervise the external reader and independent scoring only.
2. **Cover the complete question.** These E56 requests and E64 centered queries span one second, twenty 50 ms steps. Sample eight frames uniformly from first to last using rounded linear indices. Retain raw activation, inhibition, direct and associative fields with a fixed channel layout. No unprompted-baseline subtraction, per-trial rescaling or silent replacement by the last 0.8 seconds. Longer questions require a declared complete window, not truncation.
3. **Keep the inference boundary.** Inference receives only the declared raw Core activity, without additional pixels, scene categories, teacher labels, correct coordinates or answers. The world and scorer may know ground truth but must not pass it to inference or the servo. Passing through Core alone does not exclude external recognition; attribution needs separate evidence.
4. **Retain the reference architecture and fitting settings.** ActivityController has a 48-dimensional projection, one temporal Transformer layer, four heads, feedforward width 96 and dropout 0. Classification calibration uses AdamW at 0.002, weight decay 0.0001, batch at most 32, gradient clipping 1 and 1200 steps per fit. Evaluate full-training cross-entropy every 50 steps and on the final step; retain its minimum. Changes to architecture or optimization must be recorded; scattered online updates are a different method.
5. **Do not select models using formal scores.** Training-loss selection is the original calibration method, not test-answer selection. A future separate demonstration-validation set needs preregistration; formal questions cannot become that set. During offline calibration of fixed records, Core/body do not advance; retain the matching weights, optimizer and RNG at the selected point. During continuous development, do not roll back only the peripheral while leaving a later Core.
6. **Continue the individual's own learning.** E64's later calibration preserves weights, Adam and sampling RNG, combining the original 48 with the later 12 examples. Record cumulative computed and retained update counts: E64 has 2400 and 2351 respectively. Recreating an optimizer or random sampler is not equivalent to resuming it.

**Version3 boundary:** Historical calibration checkpoint selection does not authorize rolling back a developing individual’s peripheral, optimizer or RNG. Selecting an earlier lowest-loss state in a new ordinary experiment also requires explicit permission under section14. An unchanged Core-clock number does not make rollback of the complete system’s learning state continuous.

## 9. Evaluation, attribution and interpretation

**Measure actual answers and actions first.** Fit error, activity magnitude or compelling graphics do not substitute for performance. Score answers at the declared time and retain the whole process; do not retrospectively choose an accidentally correct instant. For actions, report initial state, first entry from outside, endpoint and dwell separately, preserving both successes and failures.

**Ordinary evaluation is part of the same individual’s continuous experience.** Teaching, calibration sampling, practice, development tests, correction, review and final tests follow the individual’s actual complete state. Question n’s end state continues into question n+1 and subsequent teaching. Fairness, test isolation or equal starting conditions do not justify per-question checkpoint restoration or discarding test-induced state. Test isolation separates answers, selection and training data; it does not erase experience. Rollback requires explicit user permission; necessary ablation/transplant controls use section14 branches with notifications before and after, and are never reported as ordinary continuous-exam scores.

**Distinguish kinds of generalization.** Changed positions/layouts, uncalibrated shape/color pairs, new concepts, later times and independent trained sources require separate results. E56's layout success does not establish success on all E64 unseen pairs. E64's two sets and 140 creations/restores do not increase its number of independent training seeds.

**Keep the personal peripheral fixed for attribution.** Compare normal, explicitly scoped edge silence, matched controls and rescue in complete separate copies, adding registered transplant/history comparisons when useful. Zero or mismatched activity diagnoses dependence on corresponding input, not connection causality. A separately trained no-learning or direct-sensory reader tests whether another arrangement can solve the task; it is not an intervention with the same fixed reader. Report intervention scope, remaining activity and imperfect matching.

Behavioral success does not require complete mechanistic explanation; a physical explanation does not erase the phenomenon. Attribution need not require perfect scores in principle, but must respect the current registered branch conditions. New theories or metrics do not retroactively change the project's established operational criteria.

## 10. Failure diagnosis and delivery

1. Check task and origin first: wrong individual, peripheral, channels, body/version, output task or control path.
2. Compare teaching, real-query windows, calibration examples, architecture, learning state and evaluation origin against the working procedure. Separate bundled changes and restore an evidence-backed reference first.
3. Check how the current peripheral reads fixed earlier records, then inspect actual new-condition answers/actions. Good calibration but poor formal performance calls for coverage, transfer and control checks; poor reading cannot directly establish failed Core learning.
4. Use normal correction and old-example reviews within the authorized bounded plan, then save and close at the registered stopping point. Endless training, higher resolution or blind Core tuning does not replace procedure comparison.
5. Deliver complete baseline locations, versions/curricula/peripheral descriptions, question scores and trajectories, selection criteria, actual resource records, success/failure replays and completed/stopped/unexecuted items. State unverified media status. Retry publication after publication failure, not simulation.

Update the backlog, bilingual reports and history after each batch. Date corrections while preserving old data, criteria and identities. Register new resources from actual conditions and user authorization rather than copying stale account readings or resetting accumulated budgets. This protocol does not itself start experiments, services, deployments or paid calls.

## 12. October 8, 2026 v2 addendum: correction, strengthening and retesting

After E65, the user requested feedback on wrong answers, practice containing both failed and similar examples, repeated retesting, and a new blank complete subject. These are prospective requirements; they do not imply that E65 included feedback after its final tests.

1. **Register the loop and stopping rules.** Fix initial teaching, maximum teaching/calibration per round, round limit, test roles, all-correct criterion and resource limits before running. Errors may determine subsequent lessons under a declared rule; freeze each resulting schedule before execution. Stop at independent all-correct confirmation or the registered round/resource limit. Preserve a lack of improvement for discussion rather than automatically enlarging budgets or changing Core.
2. **Record the error and give ordinary teacher feedback.** Keep every development question, original answer and target. In the continuous teaching subject, present and ask a failed question again, record its current answer, then immediately give the correct ordinary sensory statement/demonstration. If the current answer is correct, label this rehearsal of an earlier error; retain the original failure. The teacher knows truth, while inference receives only its declared sensory/Core activity inputs.
3. **Strengthen failed and similar examples.** Repeat failed items and vary a relevant factor. For shape errors, hold shape while changing color, or hold color while contrasting confusable shapes; for color errors, hold color across shapes or hold shape across colors. Include the other attribute of the same object when appropriate to check question discrimination. Record the similarity rule, actual stimuli and dose. Repetition of one image is not generalization evidence.
4. **Rehearse earlier successes.** Include previously correct examples, continue the subject's own weights, optimizer and RNG, and retain registered earlier calibration records. Report corrected errors, regressions, similar examples and fresh positions separately; do not display only selected successes.
5. **Retest continuously to guide the next round.** After teaching and declared peripheral calibration, the same individual answers the development bank continuously, using stable question IDs. Its test-end state continues into the next correction round, including newly emerging errors. Retain normal dynamics caused by the questions; do not restore a pre-test state per question or erase the exam afterward. Explicitly permitted rollback and necessary causal controls have separate identities, sources and results, with section14 notifications before and after.
6. **Keep independent evidence for all-correct claims.** Any question used to choose lessons is development data. Final positions/layouts must be absent from the current teaching and calibration; freeze the reader and score the complete bank at the fixed endpoint. If final failures later inform teaching, seal their original results and explicitly reclassify them as development data; the next confirmation uses a fresh reserved bank. State denominators, attributes, combinations and scope rather than claiming universal success.
7. **Use dense-to-sparse review.** Follow concentrated initial teaching/correction with increasingly spaced reviews. Every adjacent interval need not be strictly increasing, and a human curve need not be matched. Save actual occurrence times, intervals and counts by concept and object–attribute. Interleaved lessons contribute to spacing; additional idle gaps alone are insufficient.
8. **Register curve comparisons separately.** An Ebbinghaus forgetting curve describes retention over delay; it does not automatically prescribe a unique schedule for this model. Specify the source/formula, time units, target retention and scheduling algorithm, distinguishing a literature curve from a fit to this model. Match demonstration/question counts, ordinary stimulus dose, total duration and time since last exposure. Preserve each group's own blank complete subject, measure several retention delays and use independent held-out tests. Do not tune the curve on the final results and then claim improvement. This is a proposed comparison, not an established benefit.

First application: [E66 blank-start correction-cycle design](../../lab/explorations/2026-10-08-centered-correction-cycles/design.en.md). Version record: v2 adds the requested loop and review principles; historical E56/E64 procedures and branch gates in v1 retain their original meaning.

## 14. October12,2026 v3: continuous internal time, no ordinary-test rollback

**Treat the subject as one continuous artificial consciousness. Only with continuity in its internal simulated time and complete state can scores serve as valid results about its ongoing learning, recognition, correction and retention in this research. Throughout shape/color experiments, treat it as a human student: the student experiences one question and then the next, without being returned to a pre-exam state.** This is an operational experimental requirement, not proof that the system has subjective experience.

1. **No rollback without explicit permission.** Unless the user, Liu Dongxu, explicitly permits it, do not roll back ordinary subjects’ simulated time, Core, personal peripheral or learning/RNG state, body, mappings, readout history or other continuous state. Do not reload one checkpoint for every question, return to a pre-exam state, roll back only one component or substitute a fresh copy as the original. An assistant-written protocol, historical script, successful score or default setting does not supply that permission.
2. **Pausing is not rollback.** Pause, save, close and resume from the complete current state at the pause point. Internal time stands still while paused; discontinuous external time does not break internal continuity. Speed changes affect external duration only. Do not disguise rollback by changing displayed time, adding seconds or skipping internal events. If an interruption leaves only an earlier checkpoint, disclose lost experience and the recovery boundary instead of silently replaying and claiming seamless continuity.
3. **Notify before and after control exceptions.** Necessary restoration to a common source for ablation, transplantation and similar controls is allowed on separate, traceable complete-system branches, preserving the intact reference. Before execution, state the purpose, restoration source/time, intervention and relationship to the mainline. Afterward, report actual restorations, branches, results and deviations. This does not allow ordinary shape/color questions to be relabeled as controls to justify resets, or expand the experiment’s authorized scope.
4. **Test, correct and review continuously.** Record errors before ordinary teacher feedback and registered failed/similar-item reinforcement. Continue the next question and round from the actual current state. Tests do not secretly supply answers or select favorable formal outcomes, but question-induced normal dynamics are part of the student’s experience and must not be erased. Dense-to-sparse review still applies.
5. **Record an auditable continuous chain.** Record individual identity, source, start/end simulated times and state succession for stages/questions; internal time must not reverse within the individual. Observation or pausing may leave time unchanged; actual input and evolution follow kernel rules. Report external duration and permitted control branches separately. Complete snapshots alone, or increasing timestamp labels alone, do not establish whole-system continuity.
