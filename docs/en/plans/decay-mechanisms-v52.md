## Decision and clarification

The author requested a 5.2 version offering exponential, logarithmic and power-law decay, with a switch for the relaxation of acquired retention. Three curves by relaxation on/off produce six conditions. Suggested outcomes include retention after repetition, novelty and strong stimulation, forgetting of transient/weak stimulation, and eventual fading without review. The author authorized refinement of these evaluation criteria.

The assistant initially interpreted a naming suggestion as a request for exponential relaxation of the half-life itself. The author clarified that the request concerned terminology: half-life should not be listed alongside logarithmic and power-law mechanisms. This statement therefore does not authorize changing the existing linear relaxation into exponential relaxation.

## Terminology and version boundary

- The mechanisms are exponential decay, logarithmic decay and power-law decay. Half-life is a timescale parameter, not a third curve type alongside the latter two.
- Cross-curve comparisons use an explicitly defined decay timescale. Where curves are normalized by their first half-value time, report that time; under adaptive retention, report the current timescale and its evolution separately.
- The author's proposed “rebound” denotes relaxation of previously extended retention, which accelerates forgetting. It does not mean that connection weights rebound upward. Define this distinction when first used and retain unambiguous source-field semantics and compatibility.
- Version 5.1 offers existing retention modes and parameters, not a unified selector for these three curves. New curves need a genuine 5.2 identity, state schema and validation. Old 5.1 individuals must not silently upgrade.
- All frozen R44/R45 implementation dependencies remain unchanged while those studies run. A production-default change requires a separately reviewable result. Adding 5.2 neither replaces 5.1 automatically nor edits the author's manuscript.

## Definitions required before comparison

“Logarithmic decay” is not a unique formula. Earlier calculations used a positive inverse-logarithmic curve; retaining it requires its explicit formula and normalization. Power-law exponent and scale must likewise be fixed before formal outcomes, not chosen from successful test scores.

Evolution must compose consistently across time partitions. One advance and several smaller advances of the same unstimulated interval must produce the same state. The ages of new reinforcement contributions also need a definition; observation, saving or a new stimulus must not silently reset every old memory's age. Audit effective time, current physical weights, node retention, fast enhancement and synaptic resources separately.

Curve selection and retention relaxation are independent factors. The six groups share declared teaching, sensory dose, internal duration, formal answer windows and resource constraints. Existing 5.1 is a separately labeled historical implementation reference; different 5.2 conditions are not pooled as one default kernel's performance.

## Refined evaluation

A bounded apparatus pilot first establishes measurability and cost. Targets, delays, doses and thresholds are then frozen before the formal run. Apparatus data do not enter confirmation statistics.

1. **Retention after repetition, novelty and strong stimulation.** Test separately and record counts, total dose and spacing. Include equal-total-dose conditions. Measure both connection survival and correct cue-driven responses.
2. **Forgetting of transient weak stimulation.** Test whether its influence falls or disappears within the declared interval, avoiding indiscriminate retention. A single stimulus can be both novel and weak; cross novelty/familiarity with strength instead of creating contradictory pass criteria.
3. **Fading without review.** Record delay curves and deletion times. Label results outside feasible simulation duration as analytical extrapolations. Survival during an observation window does not mean permanence, and an extremely long predicted lifetime is not an actual long-running observation.
4. **Retention versus adaptation.** After reversal, compare relearning speed, old-rule interference and return to the old rule, measuring the costs of slower forgetting.
5. **Cost and integrity.** Report wall time, peak memory, connection/state counts and evidence volume under matched conditions. Observation frequency, pauses, saving and recovery must not alter internal dynamics.

Every target receives an explicit conclusion. More connections or longer lifetimes alone do not determine the winner. First assess the declared functional requirements, then retention, adaptation and cost. If no condition dominates, state the tradeoffs without inventing an overall champion. A default candidate needs fresh-source, fresh-material replication.
