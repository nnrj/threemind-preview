# Clock-grounded teaching and recollection

2026-10-05. Requested during E50; a separately registered follow-up, not a change to E50 criteria.

## Two different questions

E50's “following change” means whether a calibrated expression adapter can still read a taught correspondence after Core continues evolving. It does not require an untaught instance to answer what happened yesterday. The latter needs perceptible temporal references, experiences occurring with those references, and teaching of temporal words and retelling forms. Failure without that teaching cannot settle whether such concepts can be learned.

Translation of the user's clarification (Chinese original in the companion document): teach a simple observable clock, associate its readings with elapsed simulated time, and teach the sentence forms needed to recount experiences before testing “just now,” “an hour ago,” or “yesterday.”

The clock is an environmental instrument: a dial, pointer, or cycling lights changing as the environment advances, delivered through ordinary sensory adapters and the input bus. Core scheduling timestamps, file dates and probe IDs are not answer features. A teacher's temporal words and demonstration sentences are legitimate teaching, not extra Core annotations.

## Stages

1. **Short times and sentence forms.** Keep the multi-object environment. Repeatedly demonstrate “I see … now” and “I saw … just now” as objects appear with a changing clock. Initially repeat each demonstration at least ten consecutive times, then interleave with progressively spaced reviews. First distinguish current and preceding events; only then test an earlier cycle. Preserve every original output, allowing imperfect wording and subsequent correction.
2. **Where the recollection comes from.** Keep the current scene and clock reading identical while changing the preceding experience; freeze the reader. Compare sensory-only reading, no-learning copies, selective association silencing/rescue and matched silencing. Answers explainable entirely by the current clock/object are not past-experience recall.
3. **Time actually elapses.** Reverse event order, vary delays at equal dose, and change clock origin/rate. Do not collapse all input frames into one instant. Separate learned external references from temporal information already present in Core state.
4. **Longer periods and words.** After short-scale success, teach hours, days and their sentence forms. A simulated day may be shorter than a real day, with a recorded consistent conversion. “Just now” success is not automatically “yesterday” understanding, nor should untaught words be expected to have a meaning.

Finish E50 first. Freeze materials, held-out curricula, external-reader training boundaries and resource limits before the new batch. If the tool cannot express even taught examples, record and locate that obstacle rather than expanding Core teaching indefinitely. Preserve existing background and dynamics; no baseline subtraction, answer-based activity selection, or artificial past/present tags in Core. Physical clock measurements and teacher speech use ordinary senses.
