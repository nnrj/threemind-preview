# Original discussion: English translation

[Verbatim Chinese original](../../zh/plans/stimulus-intensity-and-spacetime-original.md). This translation preserves exploratory claims as the author's proposals, not established findings. The [accepted plan](stimulus-intensity-and-spacetime.md) records the subsequent decisions.

① and ②: Set strong-stimulus and novelty defaults to effective, appropriate values. I hesitate about strong stimulation being a threshold that decides when an input counts as strong and produces special consequences. It may encode a predetermined preference; novelty may do the same. I would prefer these effects to arise jointly from properties of connections and other grounded mechanisms. There is no evidence here that “strong stimulation” must be a primitive. Trauma memories or persistent responses to new experiences seem more like ordinary underlying processes behaving differently beyond some thresholds than a special built-in detector. Unlike repeated learning, this consolidation can be nearly instantaneous. Please investigate possible improvements later and discuss them with me. This period can just improve defaults and preserve these ideas in the docs.

③: Memory disappearing is expected: most short-term memories should disappear. Connections remaining while a cue is insufficient to produce a response also seems expected and resembles some human mechanisms. I consider this a very good result. ThreeMind does not seek to remember everything or guarantee that every experience remains retrievable. Forgetting is as important as remembering. I suspect attention need not be a separate mechanism: forgetting could naturally distinguish what matters, what needs attention and what can be ignored. A mind stuffed with memories could lose itself. Like a sculpture, forgetting is the chisel that carves a goddess out of stone. Without forgetting, consciousness becomes stone. I therefore do not see a major problem with forgetting; if my understanding is wrong, please discuss it and advise me.

④: For associations across time, perhaps individual characters simply have no relationship on their own. The passage of time may itself be a bodily sense. Humans infer temporal change—“time displacement”—from reference points, as with spatial displacement. A person confined without clocks or echoes may feel that days are years and lose orientation. Perhaps organisms need external stimuli to construct memories of event order. Thus, not observing order differences without common stimuli may be normal. Associations appearing with common stimuli may show a way to map time. A key gap remains, but the material for addressing it is already in the problem; the kernel may already distinguish order. Stimulus definitions and mappings need improvement.

Keep existing stimuli without explicit spacetime context, and also support stimuli carrying measured spacetime relations. These resemble the A–B and D–B constructions in earlier reports; the reports take precedence if my recollection is inaccurate. Call A, B and D stimulus elements, and A–B and D–B stimulus groups. An event is a thing changing along spacetime. Subjective time, like other stimuli, is a mapping; without that mapping there is no experienced ordering. A stone does not sense order. A time sense is an internal mapping of an objectively existing relation.

There are two equivalent interpretations at the model boundary. If a group means time–thing, A–B and D–B express B in two states at two times (the original says A and B here); A and D have an order. The direction is not prescribed: either direction can be considered, which I relate to time symmetry, though that is not the equivalence I mean. If the group means thing–time, A–B and D–B mean A and D occurring at time B. I do not mean the objective situations are identical. I mean the model does not assign these combinations an interpretation as an observer does. Without an instance mapping the relation, the combination has no such meaning. If subjective experience exists, the instance might experience order and regularity in these differences, not necessarily comprehensible to us.

Different environments, or even different simulated physical laws, may form different internal structures. Their meanings could concern temporal displacement, spatial displacement or something our language has never named, like a strange dream. An observer cannot know. Mutual understanding is possible because relations and differences map the same objective world, although internal structures may differ even under similar laws and developmental environments. Discussing a rainbow appearing and disappearing with a ThreeMind consciousness would not reveal what the other experienced internally. Meaning is an internal process.

If a passenger sees a car moving at the same speed as the bus, with no sound, roadside trees or bodily acceleration reference, the passenger may feel stationary. In certain circumstances rest and uniform straight-line motion are equivalent in our physics. If the other car slows, the passenger may feel either that the bus accelerated or that the other car slowed; I think the former is often experienced. Stimulus and meaning are distinct. Meaning is not fixed or predefined; it comes from the experience itself.

Perhaps introduce a stimulus carrier for environmental stimuli and sensory adapters, retaining individual elements. A carrier can be a group or an element. Whether to call it a “stimulus body” or “stimulus particle” is open. The kernel need not change; adapters, backgrounds and simulated environments should express these concepts. Relations among elements may allow something like time and space to emerge internally; later experiments can observe it. Simulated environments should include basic physical laws such as gravity and body senses for gravity-related effects and acceleration. Their effects need experiments. Before expecting remembered event order, we need material from which temporal distinctions can emerge. Any sustained, related stimulus carriers might supply it, but simulated physical laws and bodily senses would make experiments easier for human researchers to understand.

⑤: Order effects relying on tiny differences near thresholds may be normal. Keep this for later explanation or investigation; it may be a feature or a bug.

⑥: Background activity dominating rankings may reflect an old deviation from the design. Originally, random backgrounds should have weak mutual relations and usually disappear quickly under forgetting. After stimulus carriers are introduced, background relations might contribute to time, but unrelated inputs should not automatically produce many associations. Truly random, weak backgrounds rarely repeat enough to consolidate and should seldom form strong long-lasting traces. Earlier visualizations showed random points and lines fading within seconds, while repeated related input accumulated into regional structures. Later, even in the old kernel, some change appeared to make every input form edges to existing nodes. Backgrounds then left persistent effects and associative links. Please check whether this still happens and why; I suspect a clamp mechanism. Remove or disable it by default if confirmed.

Real hardware/software telemetry is different. Correlations and lasting relationships may be normal, perhaps resembling the physical-reference process above. These relationships could become more explicit with stimulus carriers. Their strength is an issue: backgrounds should not be too strong. But does magnitude belong to the source, sensory conversion or kernel processing? I think the source has its own magnitude, which must be separated from how strongly the model responds. Sensitivity should not be predefined; it should reflect environmental selection and accumulated properties of connections. A strong pathway—a highway—might amplify a weak input, while a weak path—a narrow trail—might respond little to a strong input. Forgetting, recovery, energy use, repeated consolidation, strong stimulation and novelty jointly shape pathways. We may not have sufficiently separated input magnitude and evoked activation. Audit whether code hardcodes stronger input as stronger activation; if so, I would prefer the network's acquired properties to determine response instead. After addressing this, observe background dominance again. My proposals may contain mistakes; discuss them with me.
