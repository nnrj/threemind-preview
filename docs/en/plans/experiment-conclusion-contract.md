# Experimental goal and conclusion contract

16 October2026, adopted following the user's explicit correction. Applies prospectively; no historical frozen criterion or score changes.

Before execution define a complete, closable scientific proposition: scope, prediction, competing explanations, necessary controls, primary endpoint, pass threshold, measurement validity, precision target, denominator and stopping rule. Known necessary tests belong in the batch. Do not substitute a convenient component and then admit that the original question remains unanswered.

Judge each goal **PASS / FAIL / INVALID MEASUREMENT**. If the interval cannot resolve a prespecified meaningful effect, state that the precision target failed; do not claim zero effect. A valid negative concludes normally. Invalid apparatus requires a separate repair version preserving failed originals.

Do not add success conditions after seeing results, change windows/denominators, escalate the required level of proof, or revoke an earlier pass. Meeting the frozen contract means PASS with an affirmative scope-defined conclusion. Report actual intervals and evidence strength; do not replace conclusions with generic disclaimers. State scope once in the question rather than introducing a larger claim at the end.

Separate known alternatives necessary to the claim (test now), prespecified out-of-scope questions (not failure conditions), and genuinely new experimental questions (new hypotheses with provenance, never retrospective score changes). Neither universal foresight nor a positive result is guaranteed.

Retain M1–M7 and close complete propositions, not merely scripts. Each report needs a goal–criterion–result–verdict–evidence table. Judge multiple goals individually; preserve independence at origin/world level and nesting of copies/questions. Definite wording never licenses hiding statistical uncertainty.

The [frozen supplement](../../../lab/explorations/2026-10-16-mechanism-discovery/closure_protocol.en.md) completes transfer/behavior, trace/elicitation/expression/relearning, and all-four cross-system evaluation. The pre-existing confirmation and later functional/ecological questions are separately registered goals, not post-result vetoes.

## 16 October 2026 amendment: scores must lead to scientific conclusions

The user identified that even a complete goal–result–verdict table can omit what an experiment means. This is a prospective report-acceptance rule. Historical reports receive separate editorial revisions; the rule is not backdated into their preregistrations.

Every complete scientific report must provide this chain of reasoning, rather than only tables, pass counts or limitations:

1. **Purpose and expectation.** State the proposition, competing explanations and expected outcomes. Distinguish preregistration, execution amendments and post-result interpretations. Missing historical thresholds must not be invented.
2. **Results and verdicts.** Retain the goal–criterion–result–verdict–evidence table, independent sources versus nested observations, assistance versus autonomy, and negative versus invalid outcomes. Scores are evidence, not a substitute for conclusions.
3. **Analysis.** Explain how controls, interventions, rescue, crossover and errors support an account, exclude a sufficient alternative, or locate the failed step. Association must not become causality. Claims separating no response from no learning require evidence that actually distinguishes them.
4. **Explicit conclusion.** Answer the original question in complete sentences: which mechanism/capability is established or supported under the registered conditions, which expectation is rejected, or which question cannot be adjudicated because measurement is invalid, execution incomplete or precision inadequate. PASS, FAIL, a difference, or a next step alone is insufficient. Name the failed proposition rather than declaring the whole project a failure.
5. **Meaning for ThreeMind.** Explain which scientific claim, research route or implementation choice the result favors or challenges, and why. One experiment may support Core learning while exposing a reader defect; interpret propositions separately instead of adding four passes and three failures into a total score. When a comparison has a winner, state the prespecified dimension and actual compared systems. Invent neither an overall score nor a ranking of all architectures.
6. **Final synthesis.** Close with a brief, definite statement of what the study added to knowledge. A score table alone does not complete delivery. Every conclusion must trace to evidence. Logical deductions or mechanism accounts added after results are allowed, but must be labeled as interpretation rather than successful preregistered prediction.

Define scope in the purpose. Larger out-of-scope claims must not become late vetoes on success; genuinely new questions become separately designed work. Clear conclusions do not license overstatement: preserve independent N, effects and intervals, failures, invalidity and incompleteness. A failed gate alone is not falsification. State exactly what the study excludes and what it establishes.

After the verdict table, include a scientific-claim/explanation–conclusion–key-evidence–meaning-for-ThreeMind table or equivalent prose. Review whether readers can answer: **What did the experiment answer? Why does that answer follow? What does it change in our understanding of ThreeMind?** Fill any gap before delivery.

Published reports receive appended editions with dates, reasons, parents and hashes. Preserve original text, criteria, data and evidence packages. Adequate reports need no stylistic rewrite.
