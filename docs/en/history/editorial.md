## Four ways to read

Modern Chinese and English editions narrate chronological volumes, supplemented by thematic histories of ideas, instruments and mechanisms. The Classical Chinese Shiji-inspired work uses annals, treatises and biographies; the Tongjian-inspired work follows calendar chronology. Both are modern literary adaptations written in 2026, not ancient sources or additional experiments.

## Source hierarchy and scope

Retain dated original dialogue, code at fixed commits, experimental designs and results. Identify later recollection, synthesis and editorial inference separately. A conversational completion claim records what was reported then; actual code needs independent verification. Event, record, commit and collection dates are distinct. An experiment folder's name need not date its entire execution. UTC+8 conversion is applied only to known timestamps.

This compilation surveyed `docs/`, `lab/` and the current Git history and read all 314 retrievable turns of the first project conversation. It did not traverse large `data/` storage or recompute every database result. Lab notes, reports, validation logs, plans and original-text appendices already performed parts of a historian's work, but no connected general history existed. The archive catalogue concerns storage rather than intellectual history. [Selected sources](sources/source-register.md) [Document inventory](sources/inventory.md)

Originals stay in place. Quotes retain wording and mark omissions; summaries are not disguised as quotations. Missing attachments, dates and motives remain explicit. Conversation evidence supplements but does not recreate missing pre-root Git history. The August large-file push failure and repository-recreation discussion explain the boundary; the earlier backup repository was not obtained in this compilation. ## Continuing the history

1. For consequential discussions, experiments, kernel/protocol changes or corrections, record event/record dates, proposer, question, previous state, alternatives, decision, reason, applicable versions, evidence and unresolved matters.
2. Save publishable originals or links before narrative synthesis. Put recollection, code and experiments side by side when they differ. Exclude credentials, unrelated private material, internal reasoning and large raw data.
3. Update both languages, milestones, relevant themes and source register. Add chronological volumes for major phases and corresponding entries to both literary works. Literary style must not alter facts.
4. Update `docs/history/catalog.json`, keeping stable chapter IDs and old links. Markdown supplies the text directly; no second database copy is required.
5. Run `tools/validate_history.py` for catalogue, language coverage and local links, plus build/reading checks when the frontend changes. Minor wording fixes do not need artificial milestones.

New evidence may revise a conclusion, with an appended account of when, why and under which conditions. Historical instance identities and reproducibility must not change while writing history.

## 16 October 2026: scientific judgment beyond the score

Important experiments enter the chronicle and thematic histories with their question, expectation, observations, reasoning and contribution to understanding. A score table does not replace a discovery history. Separate user/author claims, contemporary experimental conclusions and later editorial interpretation. Missing conclusions receive appended report editions; preserve negative, invalid and withdrawn outcomes rather than inventing old criteria or overwriting reports.
