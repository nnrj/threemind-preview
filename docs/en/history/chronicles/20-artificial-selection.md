# Volume XX Testing inheritance from the parent's own behavior

## 17 November 2026: Separating parent assessment from birth

While revising the manuscript's ring experiment, the author identified that the earlier procedure assessed candidates through newborn copies. That design selected parents according to the behavior they produced in descendants. It did not first establish the parent's own tendency and then test its transmission. The author requested complete-snapshot assessment of candidates, followed by reproduction of the selected originals, with opposite selection directions, random selection, and comparisons of all candidates in G0, G1, and G2.

This change clarifies the scientific question without deleting earlier evidence. The old protocol included both normal and swapped layouts in final tests, plus separate layout and action-replay controls. Changing layouts was not itself an error. The new primary experiment fixes the layout to compare generations under identical conditions and treats layout adaptation separately.

The author explicitly authorized a new Core 6.3 experiment. AS63-01 registered 16 independent populations, each starting with four G0 candidates. For each of two opposite directions and random selection, two parents each produce two offspring, continuing through G2. Assessment copies preserve complete state; only reproduction uses threemind.birth/2. Matched uninherited controls, every candidate, unsuccessful descendants, and complete movement records are retained.

Apparatus checks confirmed that observation frequency, host pauses, and complete save/restore did not change the final state, and that short Python/native runs agreed numerically. After the primary run started, the author authorized an additional layout control. AS63-02 was registered separately to compare swapped-layout closed-loop control with old-action replay in every G2 candidate. Its criteria cannot alter the primary study's scores.

This phase changes neither Core dynamics nor historical version identities, manuscripts, or the status of other paused revalidations. Scientific conclusions require the registered statistics and raw-evidence audit.

## 17 November 2026: Primary study completed; parent–offspring agreement without selection gain

AS63-01 completed 16 independent populations and 5,056 stages. Audits verified all 832 developed individuals, 4,224 complete-state assessment copies, 384 births, and 384 uninherited controls. No deaths or replacement materials occurred. Assessment measured the candidate's own complete state; only formal descendants underwent birth. This aligned the procedure with the newly specified scientific question.

Some existing regional tendencies continued in descendants. For region 0, 10/10 and 12/12 offspring of qualifying selected parents retained the tendency across the two transmissions; for region 1, the counts were 40/44 and 40/40. However, no population supplied qualifying selected G0 parents for both opposite directions. Consequently, 0/16 populations met both directions across both transmissions, below the registered 4/16 requirement. This is not a finding that all existing tendencies failed to persist.

The G2 gains over random selection were 0.042 and 0.127 percentage points. Progressive strengthening and the benefit of inherited states over matched blank-Core controls also missed their registered criteria. All eight mean-difference 95% upper limits were below 1 percentage point. Two populations with occupancy near 50% were continuously circling; their trajectories and behavior were retained. Parent–offspring agreement was observed, but the registered selection gains were not. Similarity alone did not establish a contribution from inherited Core states. The primary study ends as a valid negative result under its original criteria, without Core changes or additional generations. The separately registered layout control is still running and will be reported separately.

## 2026-11-18: AS63-03 locates node-ID dependence in the old ring adapter

The author requested a matched comparison of /1, current 6.3 defaults and the previously successful prefix configuration using the two old peripheral sets. Before execution, inspection showed that the old motor rule hashes node IDs into signed coefficients, while /1 allocates IDs in formation order and 6.3 uses channel codes. Identical seeds and code therefore did not ensure identical actual bindings. The study registered original and historically aligned motor rules, with blank, developed, newborn and redeveloped states and the old recorded background. Energy-model confounding was removed before formal execution; production Core code and defaults were unchanged.

All 312 stages completed. The four old /1 histories reproduced exactly at 2,400 sampled points. With peripheral 9293202, developed /1 target occupancy was 93.917% in both historical directions. Default 6.3 gave 4.417% and 95.333% under the original rule, but 94.000% in both directions after alignment. Crucially, blank 6.3 Cores supplied with the same bindings reached 93.750% in both directions. The prefix configuration did not recover opposite preferences under the original rule. The other peripheral remained near 50%, with its trajectories retained.

All 10 qualified history/configuration/output conditions retained their tendency after birth. All 12 births preserved nodes, connections and current effective weights. Fixed-input diagnostics also retained history effects in every configuration. The principal difference was therefore located in motor bindings, not established as a general regression of 6.3 learning or inheritance. Old behavior remains reproducible, but high occupancy alone cannot establish plastic-connection learning, inheritance or cumulative strengthening of regional tendencies. This attribution limit is appended in a new report; originals remain unchanged.

The archive was downloaded completely through the local API and checked member by member; bilingual reports matched on readback and the record was finalized. The author proposed a new deterministic addressing function. Recommendations separate stable identity addressing from motor readout and require evaluation of spatial, energy and event-order effects. ## 18 November 2026: Temporal differences without changing input addresses

During the addressing discussion, the author asked whether repeated identical content requires time-dependent addresses or can be distinguished through changing internal state and shared input. He authorized a small 6.3 study of continuous/separated presentation, different gaps, and connection retention after activation subsides. RT63-01 registered three schedules with equal content, dose, and final 200 ms of input. The kernel and defaults remained unchanged.

All 24 main runs and 120 diagnostics completed. Both intensities and all four anonymous encodings distinguished continuous presentation from 0.2 s and 1 s gaps. At a 30 s delay, task activation was zero and both intensities retained weight differences. Intensity 0.2 re-expressed these differences; bidirectional weight-only exchange reduced the recipient–donor response-curve gap by 99.9866–99.9927%. Intensity 0.05 failed to activate the other two task nodes under the fixed cue. Retention and expression were therefore assessed separately, without tuning or replacing the negative result.

Identical values across encodings support code-renaming consistency, not population inference from randomly varying individuals. The results support temporal distinction through internal dynamics and local plasticity at stable input addresses. Verbal counting was not tested, and no new addressing function was authorized or implemented. Inputs, complete states, shams, numerical reanalysis, and the full original archive were verified, including complete local API readback.

We retained the P1 checkpoint at exactly 1500.00 seconds and a second complete checkpoint at the actual pause, 1520.70 seconds. We checked the saved states of all 85 and 91 Cores, respectively.
