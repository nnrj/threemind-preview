## Expansion without erasing the baseline

In late September, the researcher emphasized plasticity and continuing activity while allowing ideas from Transformers. The principle broadened further: biology is a reference, not the only admissible route; candidates with physical, chemical or other testable grounds may be studied. Original hypotheses, kernels and tests must remain bound together. A new mechanism must not hide a supplied answer, and an external language model's competence must not be credited to Core. Discrete causal, continuous, membrane-potential and small Transformer candidates behaved differently. In a small comparison, 8 of 24 discrete-kernel conditions converged with the target leading. All 24 continuous conditions had a target-leading peak, but none met the old convergence rule. The membrane-potential candidate had no target-leading trial. High probabilities from the tiny Transformer concerned its trained contexts, not integrated language intelligence in ThreeMind. ## Find the cause of the combined effect

The continuous effect was then decomposed. Used pathways depleted a short-lived resource that subsequently recovered, changing propagation of later input. Removing this effect reduced 8/8 order discrimination to a same-direction-biased 4/8. Fast connection changes explained about 0.093% of the difference in that setup; local competition was not meaningfully exercised with only one incoming edge. A null result may mean a task did not engage a mechanism. A 10% threshold change also removed or reversed some effects, leaving robustness open. [E11 · 341-condition attribution](../../../../lab/explorations/2026-09-29-continuous-mechanisms/report.en.md)

“Earlier experience changes later response” thus gained a clear causal location within ThreeMind. It was not the first discovery of short-term synaptic plasticity in science. Its project contribution was identifying which implemented pathway produced this effect. Prior mechanisms do not erase local verification, and local verification does not establish field-wide novelty.

## Energy, facilitation and consolidation play different roles

The next 598-condition study compared depletion, short-term facilitation and consolidation. Depletion temporarily makes a used pathway less available; facilitation preserves a transient state affecting subsequent release; consolidation changes slower connection state. In the tested conditions, consolidation affected retention at 30 and 300 seconds, while facilitation increased one 300-second response by about 23.2%. These are parameter-specific results. [E12 · Three-mechanism comparison and discussion](../../../../lab/explorations/2026-09-29-memory-timescales/report.en.md)

With depletion disabled and facilitation plus consolidation enabled, low-amplitude internal loops persisted. Restoring depletion ended a loop in about 0.65 seconds; removing facilitation ended it in about 0.55 seconds. Disabling further consolidation alone left the established loop continuing for 15 seconds. Stopping further learning is not erasing existing structure.

Terminology mattered. The researcher preferred familiar terms such as energy, with precise scope supplied in scientific writing, over newly invented jargon. Unlimited supply was likened to unlimited food availability: it does not abolish consumption or local recovery. Supply, local depletion, inhibition and refractory state must not be substituted for one another.

## Millisecond input does not automatically improve memory

A concern that slow input concealed membrane-potential learning led to intervals of 1 second and 100, 20, 5 and 1 milliseconds. The approximately 11.1% gain at 100 milliseconds did not reach the preregistered 20% switch threshold, so subsequent work retained the original interval. The membrane candidate spiked during training but formed no connections, pointing to a timing/learning investigation rather than evidence that the entire mechanism was hopeless. [E13 · Input rate and initial embodiment](../../../../lab/explorations/2026-09-29-input-rate-and-autonomy/report.en.md)

This volume turns “the continuous kernel looks interesting” into effects that can be controlled, replayed and causally investigated. Their robustness across broader intervals, intensities and environments remained open.
