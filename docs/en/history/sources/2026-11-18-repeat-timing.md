# 18 November 2026: Repeated identical input and time-dependent addressing

## The author's question

During discussion of a new addressing function, the author noted that identical content presented at different moments still constitutes different events. Using a repeated picture separated by a flash as an example, he asked whether internal time or a time-evolving quantity should enter the mapping, while recognizing that this might prevent repeated content from reaching the same node. He also connected the question to AX/BX experiments, considering whether shared input and differing context could already support both association and distinction.

This is a summary of the present dialogue, not a verbatim quotation or an approved kernel change. The executing assistant distinguished stable input addresses from experience-dependent mappings formed by the whole network, recommending a test of the existing 6.3 before changing addressing.

## Explicit authorization

The author then authorized a simple 6.3 experiment on the three proposed questions: continuous versus separated presentation, different repetition gaps, and connection retention and re-expression after activation subsides. The original Chinese authorization is preserved in the [Chinese source entry](../../../zh/history/sources/2026-11-18-repeat-timing.md).

This authorization did not include a kernel or addressing change and did not resume other paused experimental programs.

## Design, measurements, and interpretation

Before formal execution, RT63-01 fixed two intensities, four anonymous encodings, three dose-matched schedules, and decision thresholds. The final 200 ms of input was identical across schedules. Twenty-four main subjects ran continuously to 34 s, followed by 120 separate single-probe diagnostics covering cues, no-cue controls, shams, and weight-only exchanges. Main-subject states were preserved.

At both intensities, continuous presentation and gaps of 0.2 and 1 s yielded distinguishable immediate activity. Both intensities retained weight differences after 30 s. Intensity 0.2 also expressed them under the common cue, and weight exchanges established a causal contribution. Intensity 0.05 did not re-express them under that cue; the test was not changed to replace this outcome. Values were identical across encodings, establishing consistency under code renaming rather than stochastic population replication.

The inference that new input addresses were unnecessary is limited to these measured distinctions. Verbal counting, exact interval reports, and general episodic memory were not tested.
