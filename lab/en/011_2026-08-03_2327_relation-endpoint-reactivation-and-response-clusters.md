# 011 — Relation-Endpoint Reactivation and Candidate Response Clusters

[中文](../zh/011_2026-08-03_2327_关系端点再激活与响应簇.md) | [English](011_2026-08-03_2327_relation-endpoint-reactivation-and-response-clusters.md)

**Observed:** 2026-08-03 23:27–23:32  
**Model and instruments:** V3 anonymous nodes, plaintext-label projection, temporal-window readout

## Observation

Stimuli and endpoint probes corresponding to “A is pine-nut,” its reversed form, “B is bell,” and its reversed form entered several local anonymous response sets that were similar but not identical. Previously learned relations did not all activate globally at once. Some nodes persisted through the window; others exited after propagation, decay, and inhibition.

Either endpoint could revisit a nearby local relation structure. This supports the weaker statement that existing connectivity permits two endpoint probes to enter similar response regions. It does not prove that the internal relation is semantically bidirectional; reversing a readable sentence may change its meaning.

The same probe produced related rather than identical sets, motivating a candidate response-cluster or basin hypothesis. One screenshot or run is insufficient to claim a formal attractor.

## Follow-up

- Repeat each probe at least 100 times from one snapshot; cluster by Jaccard similarity, cosine similarity, and trajectory distance.
- Compare forward, reverse, endpoint-swapped, relation-label-permuted, and unrelated conditions.
- Preregister a threshold for membership in the same response cluster and report within- versus between-cluster distances.
- Test whether shared nodes consistently play a common structural role; one labeled overlap cannot establish a “relation carrier node.”

## Interpretation boundary

The observation supports repeatable relation-local reactivation and candidate response clusters, not demonstrated role encoding, directional inference, or a classical energy attractor.
