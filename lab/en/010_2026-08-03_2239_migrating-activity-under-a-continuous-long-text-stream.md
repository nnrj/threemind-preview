# 010 — Migrating Activity under a Continuous Long-Text Stream

[中文](../zh/010_2026-08-03_2239_连续长文本流下的迁移激活.md) | [English](010_2026-08-03_2239_migrating-activity-under-a-continuous-long-text-stream.md)

**Observed:** 2026-08-03 22:39–22:46  
**Input:** a framed continuous stream of a long encyclopaedia article about artificial intelligence  
**Attachments:** screenshots in the source conversation were not exposed by the thread export

## Observation

The canvas showed activity more complex than that produced by short probes. Several highly active regions migrated, flashed, and re-entered as the stream advanced. The main topology was not rebuilt in full on every tick; the faster change primarily reflected activity propagating over a slower connection structure.

## Corrected interpretation

This was an open, continuous, ordered external-input experiment. Hotspot migration can result jointly from framing, existing-edge propagation, local inhibition, activation decay, and display projection. It is neither input-free autonomous thought nor evidence by itself that the network understood the article or formed attention.

## Required controls

1. Replay the same article from one snapshot and compare anonymous trajectories and convergence summaries.
2. Use a same-topic different text, a shuffled version, a character-frequency-matched random stream, and a sudden topic switch.
3. Measure total activation/inhibition, active-node count, activation entropy or concentration, hotspot count/lifetime/migration/revisit rate, edge count, degree distribution, clustering, components, and effective propagation range.
4. Apply short delayed probes to measure retention, interference, and forgetting.

## Interpretation boundary

Continuous long text drove rich temporally structured activity. Content-specific internal organization remains a hypothesis for controlled replay, shuffling, and topic-switch experiments.
