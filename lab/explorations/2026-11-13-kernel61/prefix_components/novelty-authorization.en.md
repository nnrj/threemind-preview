The scientific rationale was to avoid assessing novelty before a delayed associative arrival. The existing formula was retained and applied after actual arrivals. Contemporary calibration reported increases of 40.66% and 2.26% in transient target response integrals at strengths0.4 and0.8, respectively, with one-second facilitation enabled in those conditions. Extra facilitation remained disabled by default. **That experiment did not test prefix discrimination with a shared middle stimulus.** It supported a particular timing and transient-response benefit, not universal superiority. R51t5 now exposes that missing task coverage and justifies reconsidering the default. Authorization does not settle the scientific comparison; the new result does not erase the historical authorization.

Version6.1 retains `novelty_mode="arrival-window"` and supports `immediate`. Versions5.3 and6.0 preserved the same choice. The 6.1 local-deletion and sender-threshold changes did not introduce this novelty default. Immediate novelty therefore already exists as an option; the current decision concerns default selection and interaction with retention.
