## Questions and expectations

This study asks whether order expression after dense repetition persists across five teaching times, how repetition, within-pair interval and retention delay affect expression, and whether separate references produce the same order effect under equal total input dose.

The primary expectation follows the historical E21 finding: all 80 bidirectional comparisons across four material blocks, two facilitation settings, five teaching times and two retention delays express a difference corresponding to teaching order. Each comparison contrasts AX-before-BX with BX-before-AX. Net A-minus-B responses must have opposite signs and both absolute magnitudes must exceed 1e−6 activation·seconds. Recency and primacy are reported separately; neither direction is preferred for passing. Passing 80/80 confirms that dense-course order expression covers all five teaching times. Failure of this primary target is an adverse difference from an established mechanism and pauses the campaign after evidence delivery.

Sparse and separate-reference conditions report direction, magnitude and zero responses in full, alongside historical results. Historical sparse teaching passed 148/240 comparisons and separate references passed 0/64; these are historical outcomes, not numerical targets imposed on the new kernel. Expanded or reduced expression ranges will be stated explicitly. Course duration and maintenance-input count differ between repetition schedules, so conclusions concern the actual courses rather than an unisolated factor.
