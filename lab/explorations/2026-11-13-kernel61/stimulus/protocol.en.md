# R51d: stimulus units, connection formation and retention in 6.1

13 November 2026; frozen before execution. This E17 replication follows comprehensive revalidation plan v2 and the author's explicit authorization to continue on 6.1. It changes no Core and resumes no historical subject. It asks how grouping, repetition and sequence alter connections and subsequent response processes, and how weak one-off versus repeated experience is retained or deleted under the current decay rule.

## Subjects and materials

Retain E17 seeds 93001 and 93002, each with defaults and optional facilitation time constant 1 second. There are two encoded material blocks, four configuration combinations, and 264 condition records: 260 original-design conditions plus four retention copies. Conditions, copies and probes are nested observations, not independent origins. The explicit kernel is 6.1 with frozen current defaults, including deletion threshold 0.008. Copy and hash the original host telemetry, inertial-cart and gravity-pendulum records. These are declared historical external-input replays, not live host pressure. Existing random, ambient and stimulus-unit tools construct the actual schedules, which are saved before execution. Each teaching condition begins with a new blank 6.1 instance. Cue, no-cue and retention research copies begin from its complete teaching state; the intact parent is retained. Time and complete state remain continuous within each copy. These are declared causal comparisons rather than ordinary per-question restores.

For each seed/configuration combination:

- Six backgrounds, each with one teaching trajectory and two probe copies. Teach channels 1001/1002 jointly ten times at 0.1-second intervals, then 48 background frames at the same interval. Backgrounds are fresh single channels, fresh two-channel groups, 12 pairs repeated four times, fixed channels with random amplitudes, a synthetic field, and recorded host telemetry. Cue 1001 has intensity 0 or 0.5; observe 15 seconds. Equal intensity multipliers do not imply equal total doses; report per-channel dose.
- Three intensity histories, each with one teaching trajectory and four probe copies: none, one stimulus of intensity 4, or ten stimuli of intensity 1, all ending at 1 second. Cue 101 at intensity 0/0.2/1/4 and observe 5 seconds. Two equal-incident-dose conditions compare channel amplitude 1 times intensity 1 with amplitude 0.25 times intensity 4 at 0.1 seconds.
- Two physical recordings, each with five teaching conditions and two probe copies per condition. Replay four seconds twice. Conditions are complete, removal of accelerometer/gyroscope input, splitting each frame into singleton stimuli, fixed-seed shuffling, and untrained. Complete, split and shuffled conditions retain matching per-channel dose and time points. Use the position cue from frame 100 at intensity 0/0.5 and observe 5 seconds. This tests overall sequence effects; order memory after matching the final fast state belongs to later E20–E22 replication.
- One retention copy from the fresh-pair background final state. Apply an unpaired maintenance channel 999991, intensity 0.2 every 0.5 seconds for 120 seconds. A dead, unadvanced state must not be described as ongoing forgetting.

## Measurements and decisions

1. **Grouping determines connection formation.** Fresh singleton backgrounds generate no background connections; grouped pairs form corresponding directed connections. Cross-connections into the original two-node network must be absent. Split physical input produces no within-group connections, while complete input produces connections. All four configurations must meet these topology expectations. A valid contradiction is an adverse conflict with an established basic mechanism and pauses the campaign after preservation.
2. **Repetition and order change internal state.** Compare weights and response processes for repeated, single and untrained histories; compare complete and shuffled weights and trajectories at matched doses. Report strength and sequence effects by condition, without requiring exact historical numbers. If every trained history shows no connection or response difference, pause for diagnosis.
3. **Retention and expression are distinct endpoints.** Register weak-edge deletion fraction at 120 seconds, survival of repeated edges, maintenance cross-connections, death time and full weight distributions. Separately report cue-minus-no-cue activation, inhibition and direct/associative activation. The historical 57.6–68.4-second deletion time is not imposed on the new curve. The bounded target is removal of all weak edges while repeated edges survive within the original 120 seconds. Failure to delete is explicitly a negative result for that target; the known decay change does not authorize raising the threshold or extending the course.
4. **Read a process.** Retain passive temporal and activity-pattern configurations, full-node activity at 100-millisecond intervals, observer events and inputs. Determine convergence by the frozen rule; otherwise record no convergence. Process integrals and cue/no-cue differences are separate endpoints, not substitutes for the registered convergence score. No naming or behavioral accuracy is claimed.

Single stimulation, intensity and massed repetition are experimental variables here. Adding corrective or spaced teaching would change the comparison. Later ability courses retain temporal reading, continuous development, spaced review and correction.
