The study tests how expanding review intervals affect connection retention, cue-evoked transmission and language expression of new pairings. Retain sensory-encoding and teaching-order materials 17 and 29, each with spaced, reversed-spaced, massed, disabled-plasticity and untaught conditions: ten teaching individuals. Repeated ordinary teaching is expected to leave connection effects retained for 300 s; the comparison tests whether spacing remains superior to massing. If language connections after ordinary spaced teaching vanish completely at either 530 or 830 s, verify the finding and pause the campaign as an adverse mechanism difference. Individual death, observation-induced dynamics changes or unverifiable complete state also require a pause. Other relative rankings and negative language-readout outcomes receive normal complete delivery.

The primary language window remains 0.5–1 s after the cue. Each of the eight cues per checkpoint is correct only when its expected word is the uniquely occurring target word; multiple target words or none are incorrect. A paired success requires the same cue to produce the respective different taught targets after normal and reversed teaching. Report every 0–8 denominator per block and compare untaught, disabled-plasticity and massed conditions. Historical paired success was 0/8. Report improvement, repeated negative outcomes or condition differences directly; structural retention does not replace the language score.
