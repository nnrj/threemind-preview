# Review of the default transmission threshold

On 13 November 2026, after delivery of R51m, the author requested an assessment, at an appropriate stage, of whether the default transmission threshold in 6.1 should change to preserve the reversed inertial-sensory response. This authorizes assessment and recommendations. A default change will be discussed with the author after its effects have been explained.

R51m established that raising `signal_threshold` from 0.0004 to 0.004 in the same 6.1 implementation restored reversal in all four material blocks while preserving the taught response with joint-only cues. This field limits the signal transmitted by a connection; the node emission thresholds for activation and inhibition are separate settings. Restoring the historical reversal is a definite benefit. Choosing a default for the whole kernel also requires assessing other tasks.

The review will first use the current foundational, event-order and fixed-readout results to identify the critical comparisons. Any additional comparison will have a bounded, registered protocol, comparing 0.0004 and 0.004 with intact teaching references, identical materials and temporal readout. It will assess reproducible inertial reversal, weak-cue and order responses, expression of new learning through a frozen readout, selective connection effects, and computation and storage costs. These outcomes will be judged separately. An aggregate score will not conceal trade-offs, and restoring one historical effect will not change other experiments' scores or criteria.

R51l and all other frozen batches retain their registered configurations. This default-value comparison has not been conducted; neither Core nor its defaults have changed. The assessment will recommend retaining 0.0004, adopting 0.004, or selecting the threshold explicitly for a task, with evidence for the author's decision.

Sources: [R51m report](report.en.md), [revalidation coverage](../coverage.en.md).
