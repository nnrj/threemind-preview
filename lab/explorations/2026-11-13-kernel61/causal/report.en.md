# R51e: Selective connection dependence of experience-related responses in 6.1

13 November 2026. Replication of the eight-origin M1 exploration with newly created 6.1 subjects; historical originals remain intact.

**Local connections selectively carried the response differences produced by opposite teaching histories. All eight origins expressed the difference. Silencing the target connections eliminated it completely; silencing matched connections preserved it completely. Immediate restoration recovered the reference response trajectories and complete final states.** The preregistered strong selective-effect target was met, supporting continuation of the remaining mechanism and capability experiments.

## Scientific question and design

Do identifiable connections carry the effect of past experience, or would blocking any small connection set produce the same general impairment?

We retained the eight independent birth origins of M1, each with two opposite histories, yielding 16 complete teaching lines. One history paired XY with A and ZW with B; the other paired XY with B and ZW with A. Teaching ran from 1 to 17 seconds for 16 rounds at intensity 0.4, alongside an unpaired maintenance channel and a common final input. At 18 seconds, every subject received the same X cue. The fixed readout was A activation minus B activation; no trainable peripheral was present. The kernel used default inverse-logarithmic decay, deletion threshold 0.008, and separate emission thresholds. The original curriculum, source count, and observation windows were retained.

Each complete pre-cue teaching state supplied five registered research copies: intact, sham, target silencing, matched silencing, and immediate restoration, for 80 copies in total. Targets were the two edges from X/Y to their taught output; controls were the two edges from Z/W to the other output. Selection used the curriculum and pre-cue state only, matching counts, degrees, weights, and teaching-period activity. Restoration removed the mask at the same internal instant. Each copy continued its own complete state; copies were not counted as independent origins.

The primary response was mean A−B activation 0.1–0.3 seconds after the cue. Half the difference between opposite histories defined the experience effect. The preregistered target required a positive effect in every origin, at least 90% reduction after target silencing, no more than 10% reduction after matched silencing, and restoration error no greater than 10⁻¹². The 0.5-second point and the 0.5–1.5-second late window were also retained.

The two magnitudes correspond to the prospectively counterbalanced teaching order. Mean experience effect across the eight origins was 0.00246070; all effects were positive. Target silencing reduced the effect by 100%, while matched silencing reduced it by 0%. Sham and intact results were identical. All 16 teaching lines met the matching requirements: two edges in each set, identical corresponding degrees, maximum absolute mean-weight difference 0.000463523, and maximum teaching-activity difference 0.000488216, both below 0.02.

All 16 restoration/reference pairs had identical complete final states and recorded trajectories, beyond agreement on the primary score. Maximum differences from intact activity in unrelated Z/W nodes were zero for all four activity measures under target, matched, and restored conditions. Experience effects were zero at 0.5 seconds and throughout the late window; reproducible expression occurred in the preregistered early window. The full temporal record is preserved.

## Analysis, conclusion, and meaning for ThreeMind

Blocking the learned pathway linked to X eliminated the experience effect, while matched blocking preserved it and unrelated activity. Restoration reproduced the complete reference. **These local connections therefore constitute a necessary, selective, and reversibly accessible pathway by which experience affects responses in this task.** Fixed-function addressing, local deletion, and the new emission thresholds in 6.1 preserve this historical mechanism result.

The experiment used a fixed A−B rule, so changes arose from Core experience and internal pathways without a newly trained peripheral. Transplantation, event order, frozen personal readouts, and the five-system criteria comparison retain their separate targets and will proceed. The present result completes the connection-silencing part of the comprehensive replication.
