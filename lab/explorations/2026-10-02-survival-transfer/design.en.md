# E27: grasping, granular transfer and finite-energy survival selection

Preregistered 2026-10-02. Engineering calibration is in progress; numerical settings will be frozen before formal candidates start. Follows [E26](../2026-10-02-robot-language/report.en.md).

The user requires substantially longer lives, finite energy, task-dependent intake and reproduction by survivors. Colliding grains are approved instead of liquid. The primary task MUST include grasping a free cup and pouring; a pre-attached cup is allowed only after the primary experiment has no effect. Energy theft and direct competition are deferred.

Use unchanged `continuous-memory/2` and its existing energy port. Retain normal forgetting, inhibition, local consumption/recovery, novelty and the extra intensity rule. Zero reserve does not erase activity: actual Core death follows its existing post-activation quiescence rule. Never-activated instances, task failure, physical-device failure, resource exhaustion and Core death are recorded separately. Surviving individuals denied reproduction at the deadline are paused and retained, not labelled dead.

The physical environment contains a free source cup, receiving vessel, colliding grains, a table, gravity, inertia, friction and contact. A three-dimensional manipulator uses direct torque, without IK, action templates or automatic grasping for candidates. Vision, joint, tactile, proprioceptive and actual reserve measurements become anonymous sensory inputs. Object names, correct actions, answers and scores do not enter Core. Task-dependent resource delivery is an explicit external selection rule.

A separate engineering controller must first demonstrate feasible grasp/lift/transfer and validate forces, action authority and counting. Its performance is not attributed to ThreeMind, nor used as demonstrations. Grains must remain inside the receiver for the registered dwell time, with one payment per grain per batch. Spills, transient passage and passive transfer without grasping are distinguished. Any impact-failure threshold models a task device's failure, not glass fracture. Restocking is explicitly recorded, seeded and never resets the robot or Core. Mechanical joules and Core's experimental energy units remain distinct. Birth reserves and timed, task-dependent intake are fixed after engineering calibration, without rescue supply for losing candidates.

Formal target: up to 600 simulated seconds per individual, initially 16 founders, expandable to 32 only within budget and after physical/counting acceptance. Fixed wiring/environment seeds and cohorts preserve comparisons. Survivors are ranked by remaining energy for reproduction; `birth/1` inherits structure and clears instantaneous activity. At most two subsequent generations. An extinct cohort ends; dead parents are not revived as a substitute for survival.

Primary records: survival, Core death time, terminal energy, qualified transferred quantity, spills, collisions, grasp/lift evidence and mechanical work, together with raw inputs/actions, resource ledgers, lineage and complete initial/final states. Longer survival may result from lower consumption, not better transfer; interpret both jointly. If the free-cup group produces no qualified transfers, run a separately labelled pre-attached-cup comparison. It changes the task and cannot be pooled. Additional supply/frozen-learning/passive controls must retain unrelated mechanisms and be explicitly registered.

Limits: one compute process; at most 128 formal individuals, four aggregate compute wall hours, 15 wall minutes per individual; 4 GiB process RSS; retain 2 GiB available RAM, C: 8 GiB and E: 30 GiB. Combined new local/database evidence stops expansion at 8 GiB, hard stop at 10 GiB. Shared account baseline 12%, +5 percentage points stops expansion, +8 stops new simulations, refresh at least every 30 minutes. Engineering calibration is separate evidence, not additional learning samples. Retain failures/partial files and historical data.

References: [existing lifecycle](../../../docs/en/plans/energy-lifecycle.md), [MuJoCo fluid boundary](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/computation/fluid.html), [contact forces](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/APIreference/APIfunctions.html#mj-contactforce). MuJoCo's fluid-force approximations do not simulate contained liquid. This experiment tests practical performance and survival under declared conditions, without requiring a positive outcome.
