## Matched contrasts

The strong-stimulus family has three arms. Normal overall intensity is 1. Strong intensity is 2 or 3, with channel amplitude divided accordingly to match actual drive. The gain-zero arm receives the identical strong stimulus and changes only the existing birth configuration `intensity_gain` to zero. Normal versus strong measures the complete intensity effect; strong versus gain-zero isolates the dedicated connection-enhancement term under identical input. Strong-stimulus fatigue and salience remain shared in the latter contrast.

The eight conditions cross actual drives 0.4, 0.6, 0.8 and 1.0 with intensities 2 and 3. Pairing occurs at second 0.1 and teaching ends at second 1. Each configuration is selected at birth; no developing Core is replaced.

The novelty family first connects the cue and an old output at second 0.1. After 0.05 or 0.075 seconds, it pairs the cue with a new output. Compare the normal rule with `local_novelty_gain` alone set to zero. Eight conditions cross old-pair amplitude 0.4/0.8, new-pair amplitude 0.4/0.8 and the two intervals. Save complete states before the new connection, within its arrival window, after that window and at teaching end. Verify eligibility, actual associative re-entry and the awarded increment. Node first-contact gain is shared. Here novelty denotes this particular existing connection-arrival rule.

Eight birth seeds are assigned to these eight conditions. Curves and controls are paired within each condition. This explores eight stated conditions; anonymous renaming alone is not treated as diverse experience, and the study does not estimate precision over arbitrary environments.

## Retention and cue measurement

First tests at delays of 1 and 300 seconds use separate continuously developed subjects. Earlier tests do not rehearse later tests. Cue-present and cue-omission controls receive identical inputs from birth until the cue. Save the complete subject at teaching and testing boundaries. Each one-second window has 20 frames containing activation, inhibition, direct and associative activity. A separate background channel receives 0.2 every 0.5 seconds and must not connect to task nodes.

The primary activity measure subtracts the pre-cue process from the post-cue process on the new output channel, then subtracts the corresponding change in the omission control. This 80-dimensional cue effect is not task classification accuracy. Record survival, current weight and timescale of both original target connections, cue-effect RMS, its 300-second/1-second ratio and cosine similarity. Missing target connections retain zero and endpoint-absence records.

Eight conditions × six settings × five arms × two delays × cue present/omitted give 960 continuous sequences and 1,920 teaching/testing boundaries: 576 strong-stimulus and 384 novelty sequences. Nested sequences, frames and connections do not add independent N.

## Frozen prospective decisions

Engineering checks must establish matched actual drive, threshold crossing and strong gain, eligible novelty windows and actual novelty awards, non-mutating observation, and complete restoration through the next input. Engineering material is excluded from scientific N. A failed manipulation or eligibility check requires a preserved apparatus revision before formal execution, not a negative mechanism verdict.

Judge each decay setting separately:

1. **Immediate enhancement:** compared with its same-input gain-zero control, normal gain increases mean new-connection weight by more than 1e-8. Measure immediately after strong input or at second 0.31 after the novelty window. At least six of eight conditions must pass for each mechanism.
2. **Long-delay structural benefit:** at 300 seconds, normal gain preserves both original connections and raises their mean weight by at least 10% and more than 1e-6 relative to the gain-zero arm. Missing connections count as zero. Require six of eight conditions. If both arms lose the connections, conclude that the immediate increment did not become a structural benefit at this delay.
3. **Cue-activity benefit:** at 300 seconds, normal-gain cue-effect RMS exceeds 1e-4 and exceeds the gain-zero value by at least 10% and more than 1e-6. Require six of eight conditions. Report temporal shape and individual fields; a larger instrument signal is not scored as improved task accuracy.
4. **Original retention/fading targets:** separately test survival of both connections at 300 seconds, cue-effect retention of at least one tenth of early RMS with cosine at least 0.8, and conditional analytic weights below 0.008 after 30 internal days without reinforcement or damage. Apply these to normal strong and normal novelty arms. Distinguish the complete strong-versus-normal difference from the isolated gain contrast.

The first three thresholds also apply to the complete strong-versus-equal-drive-normal comparison, whose results are reported separately. Attribution to one gain rule uses the identical-input contrasts that change only that gain. Preserve every condition's numerical difference; pass counts are not population success rates over arbitrary environments.

These thresholds retain the draft criteria and are frozen after apparatus validation and before formal data. Deliver all conditions, without deleting unfavorable settings or tuning after results. Production defaults remain unchanged.
