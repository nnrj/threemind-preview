# R46b: does strong-stimulus or connection-novelty enhancement become durable retention?

First edition, 10 November 2026, generated after all 960 sequences and independent verification. [中文](stimulus-report.zh.md) · [Prospective protocol](stimulus_protocol.en.md)

## Scientific conclusion

**The strong-stimulus and connection-novelty rules genuinely strengthen connections. With the tested inverse-logarithmic decay, both increments become consistent structural benefits at 300 seconds.** Both rules produce immediate enhancement in all eight input profiles under all six settings. Each inverse-logarithmic structural contrast passes in 8/8 profiles, exceeding the preregistered 6/8 threshold. The exponential contrasts pass in 0/8; power-law strong-stimulus and novelty contrasts pass in 5/8 and 4/8, respectively.

**Stronger connections do not produce broadly larger cue-evoked activity in this assay.** The inverse-logarithmic novelty contrast reaches the activity-benefit threshold in 4/8 profiles, power-law strong stimulus in 3/8, and the remaining contrasts in 0/8. None of the six settings meets the target of a specified activity benefit in at least six profiles. This negative endpoint coexists with the positive immediate and structural results. Relaxation on and off yield the same pass counts and decisions.

## Purpose, expectation and design

This study asks whether the dedicated strong-stimulus rule and connection-arrival novelty rule strengthen new connections, and whether those increments become structural and cue-activity benefits after 300 internal seconds. The expectation is immediate enhancement under matched input; delayed tests determine how far that enhancement persists. R46a's higher-dose input did not cross the overall-intensity threshold; this follow-up uses input that actually crosses it.

Six genuine 5.2 settings cross exponential, inverse-logarithmic and power-law decay with original linear timescale relaxation enabled or disabled. Eight strong-stimulus profiles cross actual drive 0.4, 0.6, 0.8 and 1.0 with overall intensity 2 and 3, dividing channel amplitude by overall intensity to match drive. Strong versus identical-input `intensity_gain=0` isolates the dedicated connection gain. Strong versus overall intensity 1 measures the complete intensity effect, including fatigue and other accompanying changes.

Novelty first pairs a cue with an old output, then with a new output, crossing old/new amplitudes 0.4/0.8 with 50/75-ms intervals. Normal rules are compared with identical input and only `local_novelty_gain=0`. Node first-contact gain remains shared; novelty here denotes the existing post-formation connection-arrival-window rule. Complete samples preserve states around the window, actual associative re-entry and awarded gains.

Eight birth seeds correspond to eight actual input profiles. Curves, relaxation and gain arms are paired within profiles. Separate continuously developed subjects provide 1/300-second first tests and cue-present/omission controls: 960 sequences, 1,920 complete boundaries and 146,400 aggregate internal seconds. Frames, connections and sequences are nested observations. Configurations are selected at birth; ordinary development has no Core replacement, per-question rollback or reader retraining.

## Measurement and decisions

Fixed input adapters and full temporal instruments measure local mechanisms. Each one-second window records 20 frames of activation, inhibition, direct and associative activity. Subtract the pre-cue process from the post-cue process at the new output channel, then subtract the same-age, same-history omission change, producing an 80-dimensional cue effect. The complete personal-Transformer joint task remains a separate objective.

Immediate gain requires a mean connection-weight difference above 1e-8 against the identical-input gain-zero control. At 300 seconds, structural benefit requires both original connections and at least 10% higher mean weight with an absolute difference above 1e-6. Activity benefit requires cue-effect RMS above 1e-4 and a gain of at least 10% and more than 1e-6. At least six of eight profiles must pass each endpoint. ## Interpretation and meaning for ThreeMind

Identical-input comparisons that disable only the specified gain locate the effects of the two rules. With relaxation off, the strong-stimulus rule raises immediate mean connection weight by 0.0108–0.05292; connection novelty raises it by approximately 0.0166–0.0174. The assay therefore excludes failure to engage the intended gain rules as an explanation. Strong versus equal-drive ordinary stimulation yields the same pass counts, but its exact differences are stored separately because this comparison also includes fatigue and other overall-intensity effects.

The three curves lead to different consequences of enhancement. Exponential settings have lost the original target connections by 300 seconds, so their immediate increments provide no structural or process retention at that point. Normal strong-stimulus and novelty arms under inverse-logarithmic decay retain both connections and the prespecified cue process in 8/8 profiles. Gain-zero controls also retain substantial activity: persistence of a process and an additional activity benefit from a gain rule are different endpoints. With relaxation off, novelty increases cue-effect RMS in every profile by 5.80%–14.90%; four reach the 10% threshold. This is a measured small or condition-dependent benefit. The extra inverse-logarithmic strong-stimulus activity gain is at most approximately 1.34%, below 10% in every profile.

Under power-law decay, the strong-stimulus rule increases original-connection retention from 2/8 gain-zero profiles to 5/8 normal-gain profiles. The three additional retention profiles also show activity benefits above threshold. Normal novelty and novelty-gain-zero arms both retain 4/8; the retained weights increase, but the cue process gains no additional benefit of the prespecified size. Counts are identical with relaxation on and off. Actual benefit therefore depends on whether enhancement carries connections across the survival threshold and how the cue-evoked internal process uses them.

The prespecified four-field decomposition covers all 240 late-cue records. Inhibition effects are nonzero in 114 records. Among these, the highest-drive inverse-logarithmic strong-stimulus condition with overall intensity 3 also has an associative effect, once with relaxation on and once with it off. Activation and direct-activity cue differences are zero throughout. These are 20-frame process differences relative to matched cue omission. Inhibition carries most retained effects, with the two additional associative cases. The registered score continues to use the complete four-field process; decomposition explains what carries it.

Teaching-end decay timescales lie between 30 and 30.01422 seconds, close to the 30-second floor. In the strong and novelty families, 120/288 and 36/192 target connections, respectively, have no successful-associative-send timestamp; use of the remaining connections produces only small extensions. Together with R46a's repeated-course target connections remaining at 30 seconds, source inspection provides a consistent explanation: co-stimulation can raise weight, whereas timescale growth requires successful associative sending. Raising weight does not automatically extend that timescale. The measurements were specified prospectively; this interpretation of their relation to the update rules is retrospective. It identifies a testable next question: which teaching schedules turn repeated experience into sufficient successful transmission and timescale growth?

Two definite positive findings matter for ThreeMind: its existing strong-stimulus and connection-novelty mechanisms engage as intended, and inverse-logarithmic decay consistently turns both enhancements into 300-second structural benefits. Power-law decay also yields some strong-stimulus retention and activity benefits. None of the six settings meets the preregistered broad activity-benefit target, so this batch selects no default that satisfies all objectives.

The cost of retention includes forgetting. For normal strong-stimulus and novelty arms, exponential and power-law settings each meet the conditional 30-internal-day fading target in 8/8 profiles; inverse-logarithmic settings each meet it in 4/8. R46a additionally found that inverse-logarithmic decay retained weak experiences intended to fade. The companion [formula analysis](analytic-lifetimes.en.md) explains why: at a fixed timescale, this inverse-logarithmic formula can turn weight differences into very large lifetime differences. Long lifetimes are analytic properties; actual assays extend to 300 seconds, and the two forms of evidence are reported separately.

Across R44–R46, the present decision is to retain the three optional 5.2 curves and the separate relaxation switch without promoting any setting to the default. Priority goes to testing how repeated teaching engages timescale growth before choosing the complete personal-Transformer joint-task and rule-reversal comparisons. Those behavioral objectives remain open separately. A change to the growth rule would require a distinct mechanism candidate and the applicable authorization. **This batch establishes the rules' immediate enhancement and their structural-retention benefit under inverse-logarithmic decay, and rejects the prespecified expectation of broad activity enhancement for every tested setting within these eight profiles.**
