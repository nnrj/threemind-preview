## Why targeted revalidation is needed

Current 6.3 defaults restore the 6.1 resource-release order, inhibitory usage-time updates and shared-resource handling. All 128 common default parameters match, and 6.3 with the additional expiry fix disabled passes identical-input numerical-state comparisons. A version-number change alone therefore does not warrant repeating the entire campaign.

Actual defaults retain timely settlement of weak activation. The latest performance controls show that this enables existing local node-deletion rules: the weak-input workload left 129 nodes in 6.1 but one in 6.3, with 4800 deletions and subsequent recreations. This changes state evolution, not just the displayed active label. Retention, delayed retrieval, activity trajectories and closed-loop feedback may encounter this path. [Performance and cause comparison](performance_comparison/report.en.md)

Completed experiments include directly relevant measurements. R51d recorded death without continuing stimulation and structural retention at 120 seconds. R51n tested order expression at 30 and 300 seconds after task-node activation had vanished. These should be checked under actual 6.3 defaults. This identifies a validation gap; it is not an observed scientific failure of 6.3.

This review read coverage, protocols, reports and local completion receipts for eight batches; every receipt reported pending=0. It did not download the research packages again or load or advance historical subjects. Existing 6.1 conclusions and independent-source counts remain intact.

## 2026-11-15 addendum: dormant connections and endpoint reclamation

The author asked whether the expiry fix would cause dormant connections to disappear quickly. This review inspected Python/Rust source, configuration and existing evidence only; it created or advanced no subject. Default 6.1 and current 6.3 both use inverse-logarithmic decay, ordinary edge threshold 0.008 and dormant edge threshold 1e−8. Explicit dormancy and revival are both disabled by default. The fix did not change these settings. An ordinary connection that currently carries no signal must be distinguished from an explicitly sleeping edge.

Both implementations contain another deletion path: a node with zero activation, no non-sleeping incident edge and its own retention quantity, support, below the default 0.006 may be reclaimed when node retention is disabled. Reclamation explicitly removes every incident edge without requiring its weight to fall below the dormant threshold. Thus, when dormancy is enabled, endpoint reclamation may shorten dormancy by preceding the edge's own expiry. The rule already existed in 6.1; timely activation settlement in 6.3 may let previously stale nodes become eligible sooner. Source inspection establishes the path, not its frequency in natural curricula.

The preceding 129-to-one-node stress workload had zero connections throughout. It establishes isolated weak-node cleanup, not dormant-edge lifetime. We recommend adding counts of ordinary/sleeping edges, edge weights and endpoint support to the proposed checks, distinguishing edge expiry from removal through endpoint deletion, and measuring successful awakening. This remains a recommendation; no defaults, thresholds or reclamation rules changed.
