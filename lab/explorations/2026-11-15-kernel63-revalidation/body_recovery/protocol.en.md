# Persistent connections and current state in bodily responses

15 November 2026. Prerun protocol, proposed as R63p, covering E22A in comprehensive revalidation plan v2. Execution requires completed R63o delivery without a scientific stop. Frozen 6.3 remains in use, with transmission signal threshold 0.0004, deletion threshold 0.008 and other defaults unchanged.

## Questions and preregistered decisions

This batch asks two questions. How much of the response difference produced by bodily teaching under different synaptic-resource conditions transfers with persistent connection state? After further waiting, can bodily cues still elicit the taught correspondence, and how do response direction and strength change? Original E22 found a reversal one second after teaching that did not transfer completely with persistent state and returned to the taught direction after ten seconds. R63i established a different one-second direction under default 6.3. This batch directly tests persistent state and expression under the current defaults.

The exchange hypothesis predicts a causal contribution of connection weights to full-sensory responses. Weight-only exchange is compared with a same-source sham, averaging net response differences across the two physical directions within each material block. The two recipient teaching conditions are assessed separately. A consistent change of the same sign and magnitude above 1e−6 activation·s in at least three of four blocks, for at least one comparison, confirms the predicted contribution. For primary comparisons, each block uses the mean absolute recipient–donor difference across its two directions; differences above 1e−6 in at least three of four blocks identify a teaching contrast. Individual transfer classifications use the absolute recipient–donor difference for that test. Where responses are initially indistinguishable, transfer magnitude is not identifiable and is reported as an insufficient comparison condition. Taught, opposite and unresolved directions are reported separately.

For every exchange combination, the distance between the resulting response and the donor response is also reported. Reproduction of the donor response requires a post-exchange net response difference within 1e−6 activation·s of the donor, provided the original recipient–donor difference exceeds 1e−6. A decrease in distance to the donor exceeding 1e−6 activation·s, without meeting the reproduction criterion, is partial transfer. Other cases are classified as no movement toward the donor, with their actual direction and magnitude reported. These classifications are fixed before execution. Raw responses and individual conditions are disclosed rather than replaced by pooled means.

If exchanging all three persistent groups does not reproduce the donor response, the retained recipient current state contributes to expression. If it does reproduce the donor, those persistent groups suffice to transfer the measured response in that condition. Either outcome answers the question; one is not made the sole acceptable result after observation. Zero effects from node or connection history alone are normal reportable outcomes.

The waiting hypothesis predicts taught responses to the corresponding bodily cue at ten, 30 and 60 seconds after teaching. Two sensory conditions, two teaching conditions and three waits give 12 cells, each with four blocks and two physical directions. A cell passes when both directions exceed 1e−6 activation·s in at least three of four blocks. Every cell is reported separately; a short-wait score cannot substitute for a later result.

If both primary weight-exchange comparisons show no consistent effect despite distinguishable full-sensory teaching conditions, or if any waiting cell misses the retention criterion, evidence and reports are completed before pausing the campaign. Apparatus failure, incompatible references or an unidentifiable comparison require preservation and investigation, rather than being scored as a negative mechanism result. Directional changes, partial transfer and zero effects follow the prespecified classifications.

## Readout, apparatus and evidence

Bodily input lasts 0.6 s, followed by five seconds of observation. Complete observations, including activation, inhibition, direct activity and associative activity, are recorded every 20 ms. The primary measure follows original E22: trapezoidal integration over the full 5.6 s, subtracting the matching no-cue condition and then subtracting the other target from the taught target. The five-second post-input integral, raw integrals, peaks and final values are also reported. Independent 45-digit decimal arithmetic verifies the integrals.

All 16 references first undergo read-only compatibility checks. Independent encoding seed 930194 then checks restoration of all eight exchange combinations, retention of in-flight signals, same-source exchange and shams. Representative conditions compare normal observation, no observation, sparse observation, save/restore and a host pause, requiring identical final Core hashes. Formal sham inputs and activity are compared with matching R63o conditions. Tools do not advance formal references; explicit advancement of apparatus copies is recorded separately.

Inputs are fixed replays of the original physical simulation. There is no online body controller or trainable adapter in this batch, so no additional teaching or correction is inserted. The formal batch preserves 2,352 complete states: 16 source references, 128 exchanged states, three states for each of 704 diagnostics and two for each of 48 holds. Intervention receipts, bus and observer states, raw activity, delivery records and analyses are also preserved. The already verified lossless R63o XZ format may be reused without reducing frequency or precision.
