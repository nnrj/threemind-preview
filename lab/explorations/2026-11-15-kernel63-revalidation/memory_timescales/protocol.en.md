## Questions and expectations

The study asks how repeated experience leaves cue-accessible associations, distinguishing connection strengthening, immediate synaptic resources and short-term facilitation. It retains both exchanged material blocks and all eight D/F/L combinations. D denotes resource depletion, F short-term facilitation, and L strengthening of existing connections. F uses a one-second time constant when enabled and zero when disabled. Other 6.3 defaults are retained. L-off blocks the positive weight changes controlled by this existing switch; initial connection formation, short-term enhancement, node learning and forgetting continue. Retention-scale growth is disabled by default in 6.3, so L is not described as half-life growth or as all learning.

Target 1 is paired retention. Paired teaching is compared with dose-matched balanced teaching, with a single cue 1, 30 or 300 seconds after the final teaching frame. Both cues in each material block are scored against their taught targets. The historical mechanism measure is the target activation peak minus the other target's peak, minus the same contrast after balanced teaching. A value above 1e-6 is a distinguishable positive pairing effect. Default D/F/L=101 is expected to pass all four comparisons at both 30 and 300 seconds; the other combinations are reported separately. This tests whether repeated experience remains a usable association under current rules.

Target 2 separates existing connections from test-time strengthening. L remains enabled during teaching and waiting, then is disabled immediately before the cue. Both test conditions start from the same complete reference. Default 101 is expected to retain target preference and the paired-over-balanced advantage for both cues in both blocks at 30 and 300 seconds. Direction, magnitude and relative preservation are reported separately. The historical measured preservation of 99.942% is not converted into a new required threshold.

Target 3 locates the maintenance of sustained internal activity. Under historical condition 011, depletion is disabled while facilitation and strengthening are enabled. After 300 seconds, the complete reference supplies five copies: unchanged, depletion restored, facilitation disabled, both changes, and L alone disabled. No task stimulus is added during the following 15 seconds. Activity is expected to persist in unchanged and L-off copies and to end when depletion is restored or facilitation is disabled. Complete trajectories and the last nonzero time are reported. Historical termination times of 0.55 and 0.65 seconds are measurements, not mandatory values for the new version.

Target 4 tests short-term input interactions. Two three-step sequences share a middle stimulus. A→B and D→B are compared with single-cue and no-cue conditions, retaining waits of 1, 3 and 20 seconds and cue gaps of 0.1, 1 and 3 seconds. Sequence discrimination requires both prefixes to favor their respective endings within the same material block. Both exchanged blocks are reported separately. The original default-101 effect after a one-second wait is expected to remain; longer waits are also tested for improvement.

Target 5 retains the original qualified temporal-readout criterion. The historical 0.055 selection threshold and complete temporal configuration are used. Qualified target rankings are evaluated only for windows classified as converged; all other classifications retain their original names. Historically, none of 452 cued diagnostics converged. A negative result on this target is a normal completed result; mechanism peaks cannot substitute for it. The reader is fixed. No extra trainable adapter or result-dependent threshold adjustment is introduced.

Facilitation effects, D/F/L interactions, structural remnants and pre-cue activity are prespecified additional analyses. Values and directions are reported by material block. Condition copies are not independent samples, and two material blocks are not used to estimate population-level significance.
