# R63u: connection retention, cue-dependent retrieval and relearning

16 November 2026. Protocol before registration, corresponding to X2 in the original plan. Tools and apparatus must be verified before scientific execution. Production Core behavior and defaults remain unchanged.

## Questions and prior evidence

This batch asks whether connections can remain while a weak cue elicits no response and a stronger cue retrieves the learned response; whether retrieval can be transient; and whether prior teaching reduces the additional teaching required to reach the same response standard.

Historical X2 used stability-based retention in 5.1, with eight independent origins at each of four delays. Cue-dependent separation passed at 30 and 60 s; transient-versus-sustained expression separated at 1, 30 and 60 s. Target connections were deleted by 300 s. Taught and untaught individuals both reached the relearning criterion in the minimum two lessons. That measurement floor, recorded as closure-N1, cannot resolve faster learning and must not be reused as evidence of no learning benefit.

The primary 6.3 condition uses its default inverse-logarithmic decay. A paired condition uses its existing stability-retention option, corresponding to the historical X2 retention family; other 6.3 defaults remain unchanged. Both configurations share source materials and do not increase independent N. This separates retention-rule changes from version effects without restoring removed historical mechanisms.

## Origins, complete state and retention tests

Delays of 1, 30, 60 and 300 internal seconds each have eight independent origins, totaling 32. Seeds retain the original X2 formula, 1026100 + 100 × delay in seconds + origin index. Delay cohorts do not reuse tested individuals. Each origin supplies two opposite teaching histories under both configurations: 64 origin/configuration units, 128 taught parents and 128 age-matched untaught controls. Independent N remains 32 overall and eight per delay.

Initial teaching retains the original mechanism course: 16 rounds at intensity 0.4 per channel, pairing XYA/ZWB or XYB/ZWA with order counterbalanced by source, followed by the assigned retention interval. K background input has intensity 0.2 every 500 ms. Untaught controls receive only the same K and neutral input. This fixed course serves a comparable mechanism test; no trainable peripheral is calibrated.

Four declared copies of each complete parent receive no cue, X0.4, X0.8 or XY0.4 per channel. These diagnostic experiences do not enter the parent or its subsequent relearning life. Activation, inhibition, direct and associative activity are recorded every millisecond for 1.5 s. Measures retain the 110-ms answer, 0.1–0.3-s integral, 0.5–1.5-s mean, peak activity, target-connection count and current weights. The fixed readout is A−B and receives no labels or question identifiers. Internal time, channels, random state, background inputs, readout history, body and actuator state are saved together.

The original separation criteria remain unchanged. At a prespecified delay, at least six of eight origins must, under both histories, retain both target connections, show absolute weak-cue and no-cue responses ≤1e−9, and show a correctly directed strong or joint response >1e−4. Transient-versus-sustained separation requires a correct early strong-cue answer followed by an absolute late mean ≤1e−9 in at least six of eight origins. Every configuration and delay is reported. When weak cues also retrieve the response, the original separation criterion fails and the preserved ability to retrieve with a weaker cue is reported explicitly.

The target-connection count in the original criterion uses nonsleeping connections returned by the graph interface. Complete states additionally provide the counts of all stored target connections and their sleeping subset. These counts are reported separately; a sleeping connection absent from the displayed graph is not thereby deleted.

## Correcting the relearning measurement floor

Relearning uses a fixed process measure: the A−B integral over 0.1–0.3 s divided by 0.201 s. Teaching history determines the scoring direction; the readout does not receive the answer. Independent seeds 1026002 and 1026003 supply two configurations and two relations, totaling eight untaught calibration lives after background corresponding to a 30-s retention interval. Each completes 16 rounds without early stopping.

Each round delivers the current relation at intensity 0.4, waits and asks with X0.8. Waiting intervals are 2 s for the first four rounds, then 4, 8 and 16 s for successive groups of four. K background continues. Relations not yet expressed correctly are taught again in the next round, providing targeted correction. Reviews and questions continue from the current complete state.

A threshold is calibrated separately for each configuration. Let E be the maximum directed mean response in the first three rounds across its four calibration lives, and L the minimum in their last four rounds. Only when L−E>1e−5 is the fixed threshold set to (E+L)/2. All four lives must first achieve two consecutive responses above it between rounds 4 and 14. A configuration lacking this learning course is marked as having no valid relearning-speed instrument. Its retention and cue tests still proceed, but formal speed comparisons are omitted. No extra materials or alternative thresholds are tried.

Thresholds and calibration trajectories are frozen before any formal origin is born. Declared copies of taught parents and age-matched untaught individuals then undergo the same course for at most 16 rounds, stopping after two consecutive responses above the calibrated threshold. Noncompletion is coded as 17 and reported as censored. The historical 110-ms answer is retained as a secondary measure. Ordinary learning never restores a checkpoint per question.

For each delay, savings are untaught rounds minus taught rounds, averaged over the two histories within each origin. Mean savings must be at least two rounds and positive in at least six of eight origins. If more than two of eight formal untaught origins still reach criterion at the minimum two rounds, or more than two fail within 16, that speed comparison is marked insufficiently sensitive. If the parent's separate strong-cue diagnostic already meets the new standard, any advantage is described as additional-teaching savings with retained expression, not relearning after complete forgetting. Failure to meet the savings threshold is reported without increasing N or changing thresholds.

## Scientific decisions and stopping

Connection retention, cue dependence, response duration and additional teaching are answered separately. Longer retention, successful weak-cue retrieval and retrieval at 300 s are reported as observed; scores are not adjusted to reproduce the historical table.

In valid measurements, a new adverse retrieval difference is flagged if, at any historically successful delay of 1, 30 or 60 s, at least three of eight origins in the default 6.3 condition lose both correctly directed strong-cue and joint-cue responses under either history. The complete batch is preserved before pausing the campaign for discussion. The known historical relearning floor, weak-cue success and prolonged expression are interpreted separately. Invalid measurement, orphaned in-flight work, observer effects and resource or performance failures follow campaign rules.
