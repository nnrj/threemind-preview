# R63f: transfer of bodily experience across angle and gravity

## Question and expectation

After teaching pairs bodily posture with an anonymous marker, can bodily sensation alone elicit the corresponding marker response? Does that relationship persist when angle or gravity changes? Historical E18 reported the original direction in all 48 joint-only probes, the opposite direction in all 144 probes across three conditions containing inertial measurements, and no directed response without teaching. Preserve the original scoring direction and separately report correct, opposite and unmeasurable responses.

Use the 40 complete R63c parents after a one-second retention interval: four encoding blocks, two presets, and five sensory conditions (full, joint-only, dose-matched, shuffled inertial measurements, untrained). Each parent supplies explicit research copies for two postures in three domains: original angle ±0.7, new angle ±0.45, and original angle with gravity −6. These are 240 transfer copies. Interfaces, other body parameters and sensory encoding remain fixed. Copy and hash-check existing physical recordings without modifying historical subjects or repeating teaching.

## Measurement and the additional control

Each copy receives 0.4 seconds of physical measurements, a 0.2-second tail and a five-second observation period. Supply no A/B teaching marker or added probe stimulus; the actual last physical frame opens the readout window. Retain the passive temporal readout and record activation, inhibition, direct activation and associative activation throughout input and the following period.

Add one no-task-input copy per parent over the same complete 5.6 seconds, retaining only independent K maintenance stimuli. Historical E18's blank reference overlapped only 4.4 seconds; the missing tail must not be filled with zero. These 40 inexpensive controls repair that measurement gap without adding independent sources or teaching. Total: 280 new conditions and 560 new initial/final states, plus references to the 40 parents. Referencing a saved parent does not create a new independent individual.

Retain the historical convergence score for the final marker ordering. Separately score input-period activity from 0 to 0.6 seconds and subsequent activity from 0.6 to 5.6 seconds. Compute stimulated-minus-blank marker activation. The original direction requires the expected marker's peak increment above 1e−5 and its signed integral exceeding the other marker by more than 1e−6; the converse is opposite direction; otherwise direction is unmeasurable. The primary process comparison uses the five seconds after input; the input phase is a prespecified explanatory analysis. Report denominators by block, preset, sensory condition and domain.

Expect joint-only conditions to retain the original direction in all three domains, with no directed untrained response. Reproduction of the opposite direction in inertial conditions confirms that historical finding; a change to the original direction improves task performance. Never exchange answers after results. If joint-only subjects remain bidirectionally correct in the original domain but lose both new domains across all four blocks, finish the batch and pause the campaign for a clear adverse transfer difference. Measurement-invalidity and the author's other major-adverse-contradiction stopping conditions also apply.

For explicit implementation of the latter rule, loss of the original response direction in all 16 joint-only original-domain process probes also triggers preservation and a campaign pause. Selective changes between original and opposite responses are not automatically treated as failure.
