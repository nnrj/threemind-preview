# R63f: transfer of bodily experience across changes in angle and gravity

16 November 2026. All 192 process tests in taught conditions retain the taught direction; all 48 untaught tests show no measurable direction. The original, changed-angle and changed-gravity environments produce the same classifications. Every classification matches the 6.1 replication, with no new adverse contradiction.

## Purpose, expectation and methods

This batch asks whether experience pairing bodily sensations with anonymous markers allows bodily sensations alone to elicit corresponding responses, including at a new angle or gravity. Joint-only conditions were expected to retain the taught direction, and untaught controls to show no corresponding response. Conditions containing inertial sensation were scored separately as same-direction, opposite-direction or undirected, without relabeling answers after testing.

Forty complete parents from R63c had finished teaching and a one-second retention interval. They represent four encoding-material blocks, ordinary and facilitation configurations, and five conditions: full sensation, joint only, dose matched, shuffled inertia and untaught. Parents remain intact; declared research copies undergo two postures in three physical settings: original angles ±0.7, angles changed to ±0.45, and original angles with gravity changed to −6. These yield 240 probes. Forty additional copies receive no task input and supply full-duration references. There are four independent material blocks; the 280 conditions are nested observations.

Each probe receives 0.4 s of bodily measurements, a 0.2-s tail and a further 5 s of observation. A/B teaching markers are absent from test input. The independent K background follows the original schedule. Activation, inhibition, direct and associative activity are recorded. Marker-activation integrals are compared between cued and uncued copies during input, 0–0.6 s, and afterward, 0.6–5.6 s. The latter is the primary process measure. The historical readout at native convergence is retained separately.

Each denominator of 16 comprises four blocks, two configurations and two postures. The input-period classifications are also 192 same-direction and 48 undirected responses. No opposite-direction process response occurs. Ordinary and facilitation configurations agree within every condition.

The historical readout at native convergence elicits no discriminable target marker in any of the 240 probes, so that endpoint remains 0/240. Complete-process measurements detect the corresponding responses in taught conditions. This reproduces the difference between process and convergence-time readout in 6.1. The opposite-direction advantage previously seen with inertial sensation in early E18 does not recur. This matches 6.1; the subsequent R63i and closed-loop bodily studies address its cause.

Across 9,120 numerical fields matched probe by probe with 6.1, the largest difference is 4.34×10⁻¹⁹. Direction, life state and native-readout classifications are identical. Joint-only responses persist in both the original and changed settings. Neither the complete-loss nor transfer-failure stopping condition is triggered.

## Scientific conclusion and significance for ThreeMind

Bodily sensations can retrieve the correspondences established through bodily experience, and these responses remain expressed after the tested changes in angle and gravity. Taught-versus-untaught differences, opposite postures and consistent transfer across settings support their dependence on teaching history. Version 6.3 retains the bodily-association transfer capacity demonstrated in 6.1.

Two findings remain explicit: process readout succeeds while target readout at native convergence does not, and the historical inertial reversal remains absent. Subsequent studies separately examine readout and signal-transmission conditions; same-direction success here does not answer those separate questions.
