## Task and decision rules

A fixed A−B output is observed every millisecond. Each copy receives X, Y, XY and unrelated Z, W, ZW probes at total strength 0.8. At 110 milliseconds, response above 1e−4 selects the positive door, below −1e−4 selects the negative door, and otherwise produces no choice. That choice determines sensory input at 250 milliseconds; a second action is recorded at 360 milliseconds. The primary score is the first door choice. No new readout is trained.

Across the two histories, each source supplies six target and six unrelated trials per arm. The main target passes if at least six of eight sources simultaneously show donor-consistent target success of at least 75%, a gain of at least 50 percentage points over sham, unrelated loss no greater than ten points and matched-transfer target gain no greater than ten points. Persistent and fast packages separately require at least 75% target success in six of eight sources. They do not substitute for the main target. Restoration requires identical whole state and trial responses. There are 672 probes and 816 scientific whole states; independent N is eight.

A main pass supports transfer of specific experience effects through the selected local connections, including actual choice consequences. A persistent-package pass establishes sufficiency of that persistent/topological package in this task. An ordinary fast-package negative is delivered normally. A valid main result below the original six-of-eight threshold is an adverse discrepancy in a verified mechanism: preserve and deliver the batch, then pause the campaign. Invalid matching or observation is a measurement issue, not a scientific negative.
