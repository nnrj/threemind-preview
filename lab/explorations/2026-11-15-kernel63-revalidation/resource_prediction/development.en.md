# 6.3 first-response formula: read-only development check

16 November 2026. This note records preparation for M2, not a new prospective prediction experiment. No formal protocol, registration or new subjects exist for this batch yet.

The historical M2 formula used 5.1 retention and signal thresholds. Its constants cannot simply be transferred to 6.3. The new calculation reads complete pre-cue state and the proposed stimulus, then calculates source activation and inhibitory modulation at the first emission, persistent weight and short-term enhancement at that time, recovered resource release, the applicable transmission threshold and the receiver response. It projects inverse-logarithmic decay under the existing 6.3 rules without modifying or invoking a Core engine.

Its present scope requires a quiescent task-connected component, no pending signals or relevant maintenance events before the first response, no energy or spatial attenuation, and the default shared-resource and aligned-emission configuration. The predictor rejects states outside these assumptions instead of silently ignoring activity. Unconnected background nodes may continue receiving declared maintenance stimuli.

Development checks read the already delivered R63g records: eight origins, two teaching histories and four single-channel cues X/Y/Z/W, totaling 64 first responses. All directions agree; maximum amplitude error is 1.7347×10⁻¹⁸. The formula never reads post-cue observations; the checker compares its output with the existing record afterwards. Because these records have been used for development, these 64 comparisons are not preregistered prospective successes.

Formal M2 work still requires resource depletion, replenishment, matched and restoration controls, frozen scientific goals and cue materials, predictions written before each cue, and separate cohorts of eight initial and eight new origins. The transmission threshold differs from the historical version. Retaining or changing cue doses, and ensuring both expressed and unexpressed conditions, must be justified before registration, never adjusted after formal results. The historical failed prediction and later 160-test successful correction retain their original reports.
