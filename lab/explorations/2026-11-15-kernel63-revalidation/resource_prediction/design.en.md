# Adapting the M2 replication to 6.3

16 November 2026. Preparation note. This document identifies changes needed to adapt the historical protocol; execution tools and independent auditing are not yet complete, so it is not authorization to launch the study.

## What remains unchanged

Retain two cohorts of eight independent origins. The first tests resource causality and first-response prediction; the second tests the frozen formula in eight new origins at new waiting times and cue intensities. Opposite teaching histories and intervention copies remain nested within origins. Retain 16 teaching rounds, fixed A−B readout, continuous complete individuals, target and matched resource manipulations, sham treatment and exact restoration. No trainable peripheral is needed.

Read the first response at 110 ms after the cue and record activation, inhibition, direct activity and associative activity every millisecond through 150 ms. Predicting the first response before stimulation is the scientific question, so the historical 110-ms endpoint is part of that question. Process records verify arrival timing and subsequent activity; the final sample does not replace the first response.

## Differences that must be declared in advance

Historical M2 used 5.1 stability-based retention and a transmission threshold of 0.004. The 6.3 default uses inverse-logarithmic decay and a threshold of 0.0004. Reusing the old cue intensities could make previously expressed and unexpressed conditions all elicit responses. Such tests can still check numerical prediction, but they may not test the expression boundary that motivated the experiment.

A candidate design scales old cue intensities by the known threshold ratio of 0.1: causal cues 0.55/0.8 become 0.055/0.08; first-cohort prediction cues 0.47/0.57/0.63/0.73 become 0.047/0.057/0.063/0.073; second-cohort cues 0.51/0.59/0.65/0.77 become 0.051/0.059/0.065/0.077. Teaching remains at 0.4. This scaling follows a configuration difference and is not yet a validated formal design. Node emission thresholds, reinforcement and resource recovery also affect the actual boundary and must be included in the complete protocol.

Read-only development calculations illustrate the problem. In one completed R63g parent state, both 0.55 and 0.8 produce predicted first responses; both remain smaller but nonzero after the target resources are emptied. In calculations on the same state, 0.06 changes from unexpressed to expressed after replenishment, while 0.08 changes from expressed to unexpressed after depletion. These are calculations on saved state, not new subjects or M2 experimental results. They motivate choosing a useful dose range before formal testing, rather than moving doses or scoring thresholds after seeing formal outcomes.

The final protocol must still freeze origin-level criteria, coverage of zero and nonzero responses, amplitude tolerance, resource-matching calipers, the historical-dose comparisons, and the known conditions allowed in apparatus validation. If the planned range does not span the expression boundary, report that the boundary question remains insufficiently tested; high overall prediction accuracy alone does not complete every target.

## Next steps

Complete the execution and independent audit tools, compare every teaching input with the original M2 curriculum, and specify independent materials, resource budgets and treatment of known measurement failures. Then register, freeze and validate the apparatus. Each formal prediction must be written before its cue. Preserve the original M2 prediction failure, the subsequent diagnosis of omitted input gain, and the later 160-test success; do not retrospectively substitute the new formula for the old one.
