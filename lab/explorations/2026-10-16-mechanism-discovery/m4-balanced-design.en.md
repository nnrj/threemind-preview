## Question and separately closed goals

Can balanced experience produce task selectivity that generalizes across questions and has separable behavioral causal effects within a complete `/5.1` individual?

1. **Identifiable behavior:** after two fixed balanced teaching rounds, unassisted eye, hand and joint tasks must meet preregistered performance and novel-arrival denominators. Failure is a valid negative for that curriculum; it cannot establish absence of specialization.
2. **Experience-related selectivity:** select groups using development data only, then validate another taught cue vocabulary, unseen position combinations and later time blocks. Selectivity must increase relative to pre-teaching measurements with direct input, sensory class, body and time controls.
3. **Selective causation:** preserve the final complete reference and fix its personal peripherals. Independent complete research copies receive eye-group, hand-group, their matched interventions and immediate restoration. Each copy answers the entire bank continuously, without per-question restoration. Eye-group intervention should preferentially impair eye behavior, and hand-group intervention hand behavior; matched and restored controls should not reproduce that pattern.

Goals 2 and 3 together support experience-formed functional specialization with separable behavioral roles. Goal 2 alone establishes activity selectivity; the intervention result must then explicitly state why behavioral causation was not established under its registered range and precision. Instrument success is not completion of the mechanism question.

## Curriculum and information boundaries

Eight new origins receive randomized port identities with the validated optics, body and motor architecture. Eye, hand and joint teaching have equal duration and sample counts, with all three in each counterbalanced block. No E69 subject is loaded or retrained. Core, personal weights, Adam/RNG, body/weather, mapping, recent activity, executor and immutable training samples persist continuously.

A single visible object reduces shape/color recognition demands. Ordinary sensory text conveys task intent. Two synonymous cues per task are taught; group selection and validation use different cue sets. Positions, materials, shapes and cue assignments are balanced before execution. Final tasks are not chosen from current body state or correctness.

Eye demonstrations request gaze with hand holding; hand demonstrations request pointing with gaze holding; joint demonstrations request both. Teachers may know and demonstrate the target, while student inference sees only raw Core activity. **Hand MOVE supervision follows the hand/joint instruction, not correctness of gaze.** This is a declared balanced training condition, not an E69 change or a single-factor proof of WAIT causation. Autonomous execution never releases the hand, fixes the eye or copies outputs using the task label, answer or scoring gate.

The existing independent eye/hand location and MOVE/WAIT forward architecture remains. Each new subject continues its own peripheral calibration; no visual target-recognition module is added. Pretests, teaching, sampling, development and final questions all preserve current complete state. No opportunistic extra lessons, update counts or between-question body resets.
