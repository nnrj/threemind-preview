# M3: applicability, measurement and counterexamples for theoretical indicators

2026-10-16. A literature and design addendum, not an experimental score or a revision of `autonomy-criteria/1`. Do not sum these entries into a consciousness score.

The [2025 journal paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2025.10.011) remains the program's methodological reference. The itemized source fully inspected here is the authors' [2023 report v3, section 2.5, table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.08708v3). Its identifiers must not be represented as a completed audit of the later paper's final list. Earlier access limitations remain in the record. The literature considers recurrent processing, workspace organization, higher-order monitoring, attention models, prediction, and agency/embodiment; dependencies between properties preclude treating one property as sufficient proof.

The operationalizations and counterexamples below are our proposed research designs, not newly frozen tests. Untested, inapplicable and undetermined remain legitimate statuses. This document does not authorize modifying Core to match a theory.

| ID / concise topic | Applicability here | Proposed measurement | Counterexample or control |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RPT-1 Recurrence | Local propagation is a candidate, but must lie in the relevant perceptual chain | Trace causal timing; interrupt return paths with matched feedforward gain | A simple recurrent filter already has internal state |
| RPT-2 Perceptual organization | Anonymous pairing does not test whole scenes | M5 relationally preserved/scrambled scenes with matched local features and fixed decoding | Classification and co-occurrence can succeed without relational scene structure |
| GWT-1 Specialization | M4 causal functional differentiation remains open | Define groups on development data; test unseen tasks and selective interference | Receptor groups, birth energy regions and plotting clusters are confounds |
| GWT-2 Capacity and selection | Local normalization is not automatically a global workspace | Vary simultaneous input count; measure selection, capacity and cross-task interference | Clipping and saturation also create bottlenecks |
| GWT-3 Broad availability | Local transmission cannot establish global broadcasting | Test content availability and causal use across prospectively defined task groups | Activity in many places need not carry usable shared content |
| GWT-4 State-guided sequences | A two-second cue is inadequate | Hold input fixed and intervene on intermediate states in new sequential tasks | A clocked script can generate sequential operations |
| HOT-1 Generative perception | Current measurements conflate expectation, association and noise | Factor current input, prior expectation and feedback pathways | Echoes and noise are alternative explanations |
| HOT-2 Reliability monitoring | No metacognitive output is established | Match first-order difficulty; test reliability discrimination and selective impairment | Response magnitude may simply encode stimulus strength |
| HOT-3 Monitoring-guided choice | Required organization is unestablished | After HOT-2 evidence, interrupt monitoring while preserving first-order sensing | Teacher-provided answers can bypass monitoring |
| HOT-4 Sparse continuous codes | Read-only activity diagnostics are possible | Unseen stimulus gradients, geometry and perturbation stability without discarding dimensions | Sparsity and smooth numerical maps are commonplace |
| AST-1 Attention model | Eye direction does not establish an attention model | Predict attention changes before observing them; intervene on candidate predictive states | A position register describes gaze without modeling its control |
| PP-1 Predictive coding | Association alone is insufficient | Separate candidate prediction/error groups; violate expectations and intervene selectively | Correlation and template matching can look predictive |
| AE-1 Flexible feedback behavior | E69/ecology may help; tonight's anonymous tasks do not test it | Continuous reversals and competing environmental demands, controlling reader learning | Simple reinforcement/state controllers may also adapt |
| AE-2 Action–sensation model | Requires M3 closed-loop and M6 evidence | Matched replay, altered body mappings, novel consequence prediction and causal intervention | Possessing a body and receiving feedback do not establish an internal consequence model |

Prioritize the original criteria's matched causal, temporal four-cell and closed-loop tests before building demonstrations for every external indicator. M3-C1 shows that ordinary memory can produce history dependence; it constrains that component's specificity, not the remaining indicators, subjective experience or the full autonomy claim.
