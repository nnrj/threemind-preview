# R47b: 48 fresh origins confirm the delayed-retention advantage of inverse logarithmic decay

11 November 2026, first confirmatory report. [中文](report.zh.md) · [Protocol and power planning](protocol.en.md) · [Exploration](../report.en.md)

## Explicit conclusions

**Inverse logarithmic decay wins this apparatus's 300-second retention comparison, and 48 fresh origins confirm the exploratory advantage.** Its mean late history-response RMS across three courses exceeded exponential, power-law and power-exponential decay in every preregistered paired comparison. Each comparison favored inverse logarithmic decay in 48/48 origins and passed multiplicity correction. Structural retention was 48/48 for all three courses; expression retention was 48/48, 37/48 and 48/48, meeting the prespecified threshold of 36/48 per target.

**No curve meets every retention and forgetting requirement.** All four scored 0/48 for weak-experience removal at 30 seconds. Inverse logarithmic decay met the conditional unreinforced 30-day tail requirement in only 16/48 spaced-review and 14/48 strong-input origins. The local retention winner is clear. Selecting a complete default still requires addressing this measured trade-off and completing personal-adapter joint teaching. The forgetting requirements were registered before exploration; they are not new conditions added to withdraw a positive finding.

## Purpose and design

This study asks whether R47a's inverse-logarithmic advantage was confined to its original eight origins. Forty-eight new origins use seeds 261111480–261111527, with new anonymous mappings, amplitude multipliers and stimulus phases. No exploratory observations enter confirmation statistics. The task distribution, curves, H=30, A=10, retained reinforcement age and disabled timescale growth/relaxation remain unchanged. This is independent replication in fresh origins.

Massed repetition, spaced review and strong stimulation have separate continuous cohorts for 1- and 300-second tests; weak input is tested at 30 seconds. Opposite histories and cue-omission controls are paired, giving 5,376 sequences and 10,752 complete boundaries, with independent N=48. The fixed four-field instrument reads all 20 frames. No personal peripheral is trained in this batch.

The primary continuous endpoint is each origin's mean 300-second history-response RMS across the three retention courses. Three one-sided paired t tests compare inverse logarithmic decay against alternatives with Bonferroni family alpha 0.05. Prospective power was 0.8937 at standardized paired effect 0.5; N=48 was fixed before execution. S, E, W and F retain their earlier operational definitions, each requiring 36/48 successes.

The intervals and tests concern the defined activity difference in this origin population and task distribution. Raw differences are approximately 0.0003; large standardized effects partly reflect low variation across origins and are not increases in task accuracy. RMS quantifies response magnitude; E independently assesses preservation of the earlier temporal trajectory.

Inverse logarithmic spaced-review expression retention was 37/48, with a Wilson 95% interval of approximately 63.5%–86.7%; its massed and strong-input scores of 48/48 correspond to approximately 92.6%–100%. Retrospective checking found that all eleven failures arose from early RMS below 0.0001; their late magnitudes and similarities met the criteria. Original scores are retained, and these failures are not described as disappearance of late responses. The observed 37/48 meets the registered sample criterion while the interval reports population-rate precision. Exponential structural and expression retention were 0/48 in all three courses, with interval 0%–7.4%. Both power-law forms retained strong-input structure but preserved the original expression trajectory in only 13/48 origins, reproducing the distinction between structural survival and temporal expression.

F remains an analytic projection from observed 300-second weights and ages, conditional on no later reinforcement, not a 30-day run. Inverse logarithmic decay's longer tail supports retention but does not simultaneously meet the registered shorter-tail target. The weak-removal failure also replicates across 48 origins and remains a completed negative result.

## Meaning for ThreeMind and next steps

The finding that a one-weight, one-age inverse-logarithmic rule can retain experience-dependent responses has advanced from eight-origin exploration to independent confirmation. Connections shaped by continuing experience can produce stable delayed responses under the simplified representation. Inverse logarithmic decay is the supported preferred candidate for this particular retention task.

The fixed 30-second scale and current formation weights still do not remove weak experiences rapidly, and stronger retention extends the tail. This result therefore does not select a universal configuration satisfying every declared requirement. The approved next stage tests a personal temporal Transformer through initial learning, new learning, correction, reversal and return. The engineering power-law selection and global creation default /3 remain unchanged; a fifth strategy receives a separate evidence-based design discussion.
