# RT63-01: repeated identical input, temporal spacing, and connection retention

Preregistered on 18 November 2026 before scientific execution. The author authorized a small study of three questions: whether continuous versus interrupted presentation of identical input produces distinguishable internal activity; whether two gaps between repeated presentations produce distinguishable activity; and whether connections retain and express these differences after fast activation subsides. No kernel, addressing rule, production default, or historical evidence is changed.

The genuine native `threemind.continuous-memory/6.3` runs with its current defaults, except for seeds 181801–181804. Energy simulation is off by default. These four anonymous-code blocks are the replication units; two intensities and all copies are nested conditions. X consists of three simultaneously presented anonymous channels with amplitudes 1, 0.8, and 0.6. A separate K channel receives intensity 0.2 every 0.5 internal seconds, identically across conditions. There is no body, trainable peripheral, or behavioral answer. The complete apparatus comprises the Core, fixed mapping, stimulus schedule, and schedule cursor.

Continuous presentation is implemented as identical packets every 20 internal milliseconds, not as simulated continuous illumination. All schedules contain 20 frames, 400 ms of presentation, equal dose per channel, and the same final frame at 3.98 s. The presentation boundary is 4 s. Continuous input runs from 3.6 to 4.0 s; a short-gap schedule runs from 3.4 to 3.6 s and 3.8 to 4.0 s; a long-gap schedule runs from 2.6 to 2.8 s and 3.8 to 4.0 s. The gaps are 0, 0.2, and 1 s. Intensities are 0.05 and 0.2. The last 200 ms is identical in all schedules. No event-count label or timestamp is supplied as a node identity. Twenty-four main subjects run continuously to 34 s; passive observation at 4–5 s does not insert a probe.

For Q1, compare continuous input with each interrupted schedule. For Q2, compare short and long gaps. The primary immediate distance is the trapezoidal integral of the summed absolute differences between the three X activation curves at 20 ms intervals during 4–5 s. The threshold is 1e-6 activation × internal seconds. Inhibition and associative activity are secondary. Readout excludes clock values, node numbers, input logs, and touch counts.

For Q3, verify that all X activation is zero and no packet targeting X is in flight at 34 s. Report actual residual resources, fast enhancement, and fatigue separately. Compare current effective connection weights. Create separate single-probe copies from each complete state: an intensity-0.2 cue to the first X channel, no cue, a sham with cue, and two copies receiving X-internal connection weights from the other schedules. Each runs for 5 s with identical K maintenance. Delayed readout uses activation of the two X channels not directly cued, minus the corresponding no-cue trajectory. This gives 120 diagnostic copies in addition to the 24 continuous main subjects.

Weight exchange preserves recipient nodes, all other connection fields, short-term states, and mappings. Existing same-time configuration settlement and reference-side rescheduling of affected edge-deletion deadlines are declared interventions; the modified state is restored into the genuine native kernel. Shams write back their own weights using the same procedure. Sham versus unmodified trajectories must agree within 1e-10. Unequal topologies are reported and the corresponding weight-only exchange is inapplicable; missing connections are not invented. No diagnostic experience is written back into a main subject.

Each immediate comparison passes when its primary distance exceeds 1e-6. Four of four code blocks must pass at a given intensity to confirm consistency at that intensity; passing both intensities is a separate statement. No settings or windows are changed after outcomes. Q3 distinguishes weight retention (maximum effective-weight difference greater than 1e-6), delayed expression (response-curve distance greater than 1e-6), and a causal weight contribution (each direction of weight-only exchange changes the recipient's response from its sham by more than 1e-6). All three are required for the complete connection-retention-and-expression claim, with four of four blocks at the specified intensity. Movement toward the donor is also reported, without requiring full replacement of its history.

Death, residual X activation, in-flight task signals, cross-connections to K, unequal doses, or failed shams invalidate the relevant measurements. Valid subthreshold results remain negative for the specified endpoint, not a universal claim of no memory. These tests do not measure counting, verbal interval reports, or episodic recollection.
