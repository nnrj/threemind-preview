# OCEAN63-P1. Food supply, contact predation and reproduction across generations

21 November 2026. The author proposed a longer interval between new grains, consumption of a smaller individual as soon as the two bodies touch, and multiple offspring per birth. We ask whether predation becomes more frequent in later generations under sparse food supply and whether early hunters leave more surviving descendants. We also track turns towards grains, turns towards live prey and turns away from a larger individual on a collision course.

## Habitats and individuals

The four preceding studies close and their originals undergo verification first. The researchers then add a retrospective turning analysis to their reports. P1 starts afterwards with native threemind.continuous-memory/6.3 individuals and the existing sensory and motor peripherals. The new environmental rules have the identifier threemind.ocean-volume-contact-litter/1.

Two independent habitat seeds, 471101 and 471102, use peripheral seeds 472100 and 472200. Each habitat starts with 53 founders with blank Cores and 53 grains. The two worlds in each pair have identical individuals, food, mappings and random state at the start. The reference world receives one new grain per simulated second and the sparse world one every eight seconds. Starting with 53 grains reduces the carryover from the earlier design, which started with 360. Both arms use the same contact and birth rules. Observe each world to 2,400 simulated seconds or extinction. Retain observations at 600, 900, 1,500 and 2,400 seconds and record the highest generation reached. The independent unit is a habitat. Individuals, matched conditions and generations are nested within habitats.

The planar world uses the existing displaced-volume formula (4πr³/3), where r is the area-equivalent body radius. Every 20 simulated milliseconds, the apparatus checks the physical surfaces of capsules, respecting periodic boundaries. At the first detected touch or overlap, the larger individual consumes the smaller one. Equal-volume individuals simply collide. For simultaneous events, process the largest predator first, then the closest prey for that predator, using identity only to resolve ties. Kin follow the same rule. Report parent–offspring and sibling predation separately. Bodies left by natural deaths remain edible. Live prey provide body reserves and material paid for during construction at the existing digestive efficiency. Available reserves pay the shell cost first, followed by energy absorbed in the meal. Record any unpaid cost. A kill can yield zero energy or a net loss.

Each litter contains two offspring. The parent must fund both Cores, both bodies, construction and reproductive costs, and retain its minimum reserve. If energy or capacity is insufficient, defer the entire litter. Each offspring uses threemind.birth/2, inherits persistent network state and decay age, starts with cleared activity at internal time zero, and receives its own body-trait draw under the existing mutation rules. Siblings start on opposite sides of the parent along a random axis with clearance at birth. There is no subsequent kin protection. Observe parentage, litter, generation, birth and death; record births deferred by the limits of 97 living bodies and 1,024 total births.

## Outcomes

1. **Predation over generations.** For each generation, count kills, individual hunters, repeat hunters and actual living time. Compare generations 1–2 with 3–5 using live kills per 100 living-body minutes within each individual's first 180 seconds of life. A positive change in both sparse worlds supports increased predation over generations. A larger increase than in the reference world in both habitat pairs supports an additional effect of sparse supply. Report realized generations, missing denominators, rates excluding parent–offspring and sibling kills, grain intake and scavenging.
2. **Surviving descendants.** Classify naturally born individuals that survived to age 60 by whether they themselves consumed live prey before age 60. Count direct offspring born at parental ages 60 to under 180 seconds that themselves survived to 60 seconds. Include eligible individuals with no offspring. Require follow-up to parental age 240 seconds or world extinction. Separately report early deaths, incomplete follow-up, all births, living direct offspring and all living descendants at parental age 240. Compare individuals within habitat, supply, generation and size at birth. Use radius relative to 0.008 m and boundaries of 0.85, 1 and 1.15. Weight each stratum containing both groups by the size of its smaller group. A positive adjusted difference in both sparse worlds supports a reproductive advantage associated with early predation. Also report unadjusted differences and lineage counts.
3. **Turning to food.** For grains use the original E37/E38 five-second screen, excluding the ingestion sample. The water-relative direction must change by at least 30°, final alignment with the local nutrient gradient must reach 0.7 and improve by at least 0.3. Mean water-relative speed must reach 0.001 m/s, mean absolute turn command 0.01, and the mean product of turn command and angular velocity must be positive. Require a collision-free window and suppress duplicate hits by the same individual within five seconds. For live prey use positions recorded every 0.2 seconds over the preceding five seconds, the direction towards the actual prey, the same turn, command and alignment criteria, and a decrease in distance of at least 5 mm. Preserve identity, event time and original segment.
4. **Evasive turning.** Start observing a pair when a larger individual comes within three sensory ranges of a smaller one, capped at 0.06 m, and they have closed by at least 2 mm during the preceding second. Sample the same pair at most once every five seconds. Both must remain observable for the next five seconds. The smaller individual must survive, turn by at least 30° relative to the water with a matching motor command and angular velocity, clear the collision screen, align at least 0.7 with the direction away from the larger body and finish at least 5 mm farther away. Keep the larger individual's observed track and extrapolate the smaller individual's preceding ground velocity at fixed orientation. Count an evasive turn when that straight course would have led to physical contact. This geometric comparison is an observer measure. For each type of turn, report events, identities, repeats and independent habitats. One qualifying event establishes occurrence in that habitat; occurrence in both establishes replication across the two habitats.
