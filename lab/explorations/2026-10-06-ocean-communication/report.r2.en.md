**Sound changes other individuals' behavior, and newly formed auditory connections participate in action. However, the original apparatus submitted body and ear observations separately, preventing connections between them in this configuration. A separate protocol declaring a common bodily sample allows those connections to form naturally, without modifying Core.**

Human-language training is paused; existing Transformers, kernels and experiments remain intact. There are 22 completed simulation stages plus one preserved zero-advance monitoring pause, totaling 75.70 minutes of simulation computation. All controls retain complete oceans, organisms, nutrients and random renewal.

## 1. Answers established this round

| Question | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Can one individual's output reach another's input through the environment? | **Yes.** OutputBus → mouth → medium → ears → InputBus is implemented with actual input evidence. |
| Does it change behavior? | **Yes.** Muting others changes actions in 39/42 and 38/40 individuals during the first second, beginning at the next 20 ms sample. |
| Do acquired auditory connections contribute? | **Yes, within a limited scope.** Silencing selected auditory edges affects actions more than equal-count/equal-weight nonauditory controls; immediate release restores five seconds of action/path equality. These are auditory-internal edges, not sound-to-body/food associations. |
| Is there an ecological benefit? | **A modest feeding benefit in both 40-second main windows, not yet a stable, learned communication advantage.** Short-window direction can reverse; predation and population do not agree consistently. |
| Has language, recollection or reasoning been established? | **No.** Cross-context reference, absent-event and unseen-combination tests were not completed. Changed actions alone are not language. |
| Is this direction worth continuing? | **Yes, with a specific next step: identify the contribution of cross-sensory connections under common sampling.** Neither a larger Transformer nor an immediate Core change is required. |

## 2. World and controls

Each periodic 1.2 m ocean starts with 37 bodies, capacity 67, 360 nutrients, random renewal of one per second and nutrient capacity 4096. Three archived E38 candidates—two turn/feed candidates and one consumer—are copied 12–13 times each. Structural memory/body traits remain, fast activity is cleared and ordinary founder energy is supplied. Historical `continuous-memory/2` identity/configuration remains, including enabled fast-trace/context mechanisms. This is not blank-brain evolution from scratch.

Water/current, temperature, collision and bodily feedback, finite energy, movement costs, mutation, birth, activity death, shells, partial-enclosure consumption and bilateral immature parent protection remain. Same-source compatible bodies cannot consume one another; other sources can. Compatible contact lasting one second permits lossy sharing of newly acquired energy. **This is an environmental sharing rule, not proof of voluntary cooperation or joint hunting.** No pursuit strategy or multi-body capture solver was introduced.

The inherited oscillator mouth samples at 100 ms, using at most 12 activity sites above 0.08 activation. Anonymous frequencies undergo attenuation and finite delay, reaching eight bands per ear with fixed gain 0.12. Emission costs body energy. Identity, cohort names, food coordinates and evaluative labels remain outside Core. This is a band-energy approximation, not full underwater acoustics or PCM simulation.

Main controls branch from each complete world at 150 seconds and continue 40 seconds: live, other-muted and freshly scrambled other-frequency bands. Self-hearing and emission costs remain. Scrambling preserves each arrival's acoustic power, not necessarily the total after nonlinear sensory conversion or after trajectories diverge. The two environmental seeds are the two replication sources; cloned organisms, forks and time samples are not independent trials.

## 3. Original /1: behavioral influence, incomplete attribution of utility

### Forty-second ecological results

| 1 | Live | 60 | 2 | 3 | 43 |

Live feeding exceeds muted by approximately 7.1% and 8.3%. But environment 1's first 20 seconds are 26 versus 33, reversing the 40-second direction. A favorable endpoint does not establish stable benefit. Cohort live-minus-muted food is +2/+7 for turn44, +6/+8 for engulf15 and −4/−10 for turn59. Predation and population are different outcomes.

### Actions and connection interventions

Select the strongest 64 auditory-related edges per living individual: 2,688/2,560 total. Nonauditory controls match count and total weight exactly; every selected edge has weight 1. Topological position, degree, history and activity are not fully matched.

| Mean first-second action difference from live | Environment 1 | Environment 2 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Other-muted | 0.052784 | 0.042474 |
| Other-scrambled | 0.040239 | 0.043413 |
| Auditory edges silenced | 0.002477 | 0.003938 |
| Matched nonauditory edges silenced | 0.001131 | 0.001132 |
| Silenced then immediately released | 0 | 0 |

The quantity is Euclidean distance between existing three-component action vectors, not intelligence. Auditory-edge effects are about 2.2 and 3.5 times the matched effects, showing these newly formed connections participate in behavior. Rescue releases the mask immediately at the same starting state; it does not reverse an already diverged ecosystem.

Twenty-second food counts for live/auditory-mask/matched-mask are 26/28/27 and 37/37/37. **The selected 64 edges do not explain away the feeding benefit.** At the earlier 20-second checkpoint in environment 1, muting already changes behavior, but both branches eat 20 nutrients over five seconds. Innate projection, auditory learning and self-hearing contributions have not been fully separated.

### Where learning was constrained

At 150 seconds, living individuals have 9,830/9,432 auditory-related edges, but **zero edges connecting auditory and nonauditory nodes in either world**. Every selected target edge is auditory-internal.

The apparatus submits body and hearing as separate Stimulus groups despite their shared physical sampling time. This configuration builds candidate edges from each jointly touched group. Sounds and concurrent bodily experience therefore never enter the same candidate group. Motor projection, energy and other state coupling can still change behavior, but the intended cross-sensory connection learning is constrained.

## 4. Supplemental /2: physical common sampling enables cross-sensory learning

Retain /1 and every result. New world/state `/2` combines actual body and arriving ear measurements from **one body at one physical sample**. Each anonymous channel/value remains; no answer or Core formula is added. New independent worlds are copied from the original time-zero sources; old worlds are not upgraded in place.

The grouping is not dynamically equivalent to /1: joint edge candidates and existing local competition can both change. Outcome changes cannot all be attributed to cross-sensory edges without targeted intervention.

Each new environment develops for 30 seconds with a checkpoint at 20. An other-muted ten-second branch is compared with the live world's 20–30-second segment:

| Measurement | Environment 1 | Environment 2 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Living at 20 seconds | 34 | 36 |
| Living individuals with auditory–nonauditory edges | 34/34 | 36/36 |
| Total cross-sensory edges in living individuals | 27,456 | 29,560 |
| Live/muted food during 20–30 seconds | 25/24 | 27/25 |
| Individuals with first-second action changes after muting | 32/34 | 36/36 |

**The useful advance is removing an apparatus barrier to connection formation—not treating more edges as language.** There is no obvious group functional collapse, and modest short-window feeding differences remain. However, this is a 30-second pilot with no selective cross-edge ablation; stable food/danger reference is unproven. Its totals cannot be compared directly with /1's 150–190-second ecosystem.

## 5. Internal activity, expression and next steps

Internal activity and public expression need not be preclassified by human semantics, but they have different physical reach. Researchers may observe internal activity; other organisms receive only bodily emissions. The current mouth passively sonifies stronger activity. Voluntary decisions about when to speak and to whom have not been established. Original sound spectra change slowly overall, potentially conveying body/activity state or merely a sustained background; they should not be named food/help words in advance.

Recommended next steps, not automatically executed:

1. In /2, selectively silence cross-sensory regions with matched controls/rescue and combine connection silencing with other-muting to distinguish partner effects from self-hearing.
2. Retain complete oceans while introducing physical information differences—limited visibility and changing nutrient patches—so a receiver can potentially benefit from a partner's experience. Never encode the food direction or correct message for Core.
3. Reverse environmental correspondences and test new positions. If responses change with experience and targeted connections mediate that change, this supports learned communicative relations.
4. Only then test absent events, delayed recollection and novel combinations. A Transformer may later assist observer translation. A controllable bodily emission gate is a candidate, not a semantic gatekeeper added to Core.

**Current conclusion: physical signaling, behavioral influence and some connection contributions are established; the common-observation barrier is repaired. Learned transferable communication, recollection and reasoning remain to be tested.**
